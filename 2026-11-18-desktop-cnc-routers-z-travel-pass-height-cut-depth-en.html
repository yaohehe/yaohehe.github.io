In three lines:

  • The third number in the working-area cell is travel. "How thick a board fits" is pass height. "How deep one pass cuts" is depth of cut. None converts into the others, and official pages mix them freely.
  • Pass height can exceed travel: 100 mm against 78 mm on the 4040-PRO MAX, 130 mm against 120 mm on the PROVerXL 4030S. The extra distance is how far the spindle can be raised, not how far the bit can descend.
  • The 710W and 75W figures on one page are two different parts: the 710W trimmer runs off mains, while the 24V/4A supply (96 W) feeds steppers and the control board.

One machine, one page, four working areas

Start with the worst case. The Amazon listing for the 4040-PRO carries four different area figures on a single ASIN:

  • bullet one: 15.7" × 15.7" × 3.1"
  • an A+ module: 435 × 400 × 80 mm (17.13 × 15.75 × 3.15 in)
  • a comparison carousel title: 17.1" × 15.7" × 3.1"
  • the technical table: "XY Axis Effective Working Area 15.7 × 15.7 inch" and "Z Axis Effective Working Range 3.1 inch"

That is 35 mm of disagreement on X, and 1.3 mm on Z between 80 mm and 3.1 inches (78.7 mm). None of the four answers the question buyers actually have, which is how thick a board fits.

The brand's own spec table lists 400 × 400 × 78 mm, matching the Amazon technical table. So the 435 × 400 × 80 figure belongs to the marketing module only — not a fabrication, just the same machine described by different people in different modules, with nothing telling you which one to trust.

Four names for the third number

Lay the official vocabulary out and each term maps to one physical quantity:

  • Travel: how far the Z axis moves between its lowest and highest points. This is what the third cell of a working-area figure actually reports.
  • Pass height / clearance: the vertical gap between the table and the lowest point of the spindle (or bit tip) at its highest position. Stock, spoilboard, clamps and bit protrusion all have to fit inside it.
  • Depth of cut: how much material one pass removes. It depends on spindle power, rigidity, feed rate and bit diameter — a process parameter, not a machine parameter.
  • Stock thickness: the workpiece itself. It appears in no spec table, yet it decides whether the job is possible.

The official mixing is real. The 3018-PRO product page says "The effective engraving depth is 1.8 inches" (about 45.7 mm), and its working-area cell also reads 1.8 inches (45 mm) — a word with "depth" in it, used to describe travel. The 4040-PRO MAX takes the other route: 78 mm in the area cell, a separate 100 mm pass-height line, and a Q&A note that the 100 mm is clearance.

The only operational takeaway: when you see the third number, ask whether it is travel or clearance.

Why can clearance exceed travel? Because the spindle can be mounted high or low on its carriage. Slide the mount up a notch and the bit tip sits further from the table: clearance grows, travel does not, and the deepest cut the machine can reach does not change either.

The reverse also happens — mount the spindle low and clearance shrinks below travel. There is no fixed relationship between the two, and the number a listing quotes depends on which mounting position the photos were taken in.

The 71 mm implied by "40% higher than the PRO"

The official 4040-PRO MAX copy reads "a massive 100mm Z-pass height (40% higher than the PRO)". Work backwards: 100 ÷ 1.4 ≈ 71.4 mm.

The 4040-PRO spec table, meanwhile, lists its working area as 400 × 400 × 78 mm.

That is a 6.6 mm gap. Two explanations fit: the 40% is rounded (71 and 78 could be pre-rounding values), or the 78 mm cell is not pass height at all. I cannot settle which, because no official page states both the PRO's travel and its clearance.

Referring to a sibling model as a benchmark is common in spec sheets, but when the benchmark itself is undefined, the number you extract is an order of magnitude at best. If pass height decides your purchase, buy the machine whose page actually prints pass height.

75W and 710W: two parts on one page

The 4040-PRO listing shows two spindle power figures, and readers routinely assume one is a typo:

  • the bullet claims "robust 710W power output" and "6500-30000 RPM"
  • the technical table says "Spindle Motor = 75W"

Both are correct, for different objects. The brand's spec sheet lists Spindle Power 75W (Upgradable), Max RPM 9000, Spindle Mount 42mm & 52mm — that is the small spindle on the frame. The 710W, 6500–30000 RPM, 65 mm body is a trimmer router added by the bundle.

The difference is not only wattage. The frame clamps at 42/52 mm; the trimmer needs 65 mm, so fitting it means changing the Z-axis module. The brand sells that module separately, titled "XZ-Axis Module of 4040-PRO ... for Carving Metal Wood Acrylic" — a hardware gate on the 75W-to-710W path that never appears in the main spec table.

Now look at the supply line. Both the 4040-PRO and the 4040-PRO MAX list 24V/4A, which is 96 W; a 710W spindle cannot run on 96 W. The PROVerXL 4030S page spells the split out: power supply "AC 110/230V, 48V 7A & 24V 8A", spindle "710W AC Spindle". Two supplies, two parts; the 24V rail feeds steppers and the board.

So when a listing says "power supply 12V/5A" or "24V/4A", do not convert that cell into cutting capability. It belongs to a different system than the spindle.

The collet line: what bit shanks fit on day one

The 4040-PRO and 4040-PRO MAX box lists include exactly two collets: ER11 4 mm and ER11 3.175 mm. The bullet, meanwhile, says "Compatible with shank diameters ranging from 1-8mm to 1/4", but please note that 1/4" and 1-8mm collets are not in[cluded]".

In plain terms: out of the box you can hold 3.175 mm and 4 mm shanks. Anything at 6 mm or 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) needs a collet you buy separately.

Why shank diameter matters shows up in two calculations.

Surface speed v = π × D × n ÷ 60. At 20000 RPM, a 3.175 mm bit runs at π × 0.003175 × 20000 ÷ 60 ≈ 3.32 m/s; a 6 mm bit runs at 6.28 m/s, nearly double (arithmetic). Surface speed maps to edge temperature, so doubling bit diameter is not "cutting faster", it is "running hotter".

Chip load = feed rate ÷ (RPM × flutes). Taking two flutes and official feed figures:

  • 3018 REX: 3000 mm/min ÷ (10000 RPM × 2) ≈ 0.15 mm per tooth
  • 4040-PRO: 2000 mm/min ÷ (9000 RPM × 2) ≈ 0.11 mm per tooth
  • PROVerXL 4030S: 5000 mm/min ÷ (30000 RPM × 2) ≈ 0.08 mm per tooth

That is a 1.8× spread in chip load (arithmetic). Higher RPM does not mean faster cutting: the 4030S spins three times faster than the 3018 REX and takes under 60% of the chip per tooth. This is the number that decides whether you hear a steady hiss or a scream plus scorched edges.

A 400 × 400 table on 12V/5A: power is not the bottleneck

FoxAlien's official specs for the Masuter Pro: working area 400 × 400 × 60 mm, 775 spindle at 10000 RPM, input 12V/5A, steppers rated 1.3 A, 12 V, 0.25 Nm.

12 V × 5 A = 60 W moving a 400 × 400 mm table. The 4040-PRO runs 24V/4A (96 W) with a 75W spindle and weighs 20.65 kg; the 4040-PRO MAX also runs 24V/4A at 24.6 kg.

Set the three side by side and a pattern appears: table size scales with structure weight, not with supply wattage. At the 400 × 400 class, 60 W and 96 W both move the axes fine; what decides whether you can cut is frame rigidity, rail type and spindle — not the voltage and current cell.

FAQ

Q: The working area says 400 × 400 × 78 — can I cut a 78 mm board?

No. 78 mm is Z travel, not cutting thickness, and not clearance. Fitting a 78 mm blank means stock, spoilboard, clamps and bit protrusion all inside the clearance (100 mm on the 4040-PRO MAX), and then the machine still has to be able to cut it. The brand's own wording is that pass height describes clearance, not cutting depth or Z-axis travel.

Q: The same page says 710W and 75W. Which is real?

Both, on two different parts. The frame carries a 75W spindle at 9000 RPM with 42/52 mm mounts; the bundle adds a 710W trimmer at 6500–30000 RPM with a 65 mm body. Fitting that trimmer to the frame requires a different Z module.

Q: For aluminium, which number matters?

Structure first, wattage second. Linear guides and ball screws, total machine weight and mount diameter govern vibration; power governs whether feed can be sustained. Brands describe metal capability as soft metals or soft aluminium, and 3018-class accuracy of ±0.1 mm means leaving stock for a finishing pass.

Q: Is 40 mm of Z enough?

Do the arithmetic. A 20 mm blank plus a 10 mm clamp leaves 10 mm for bit protrusion and safe height — fine for shallow reliefs and cutting through thin sheet, tight for deep pockets or 3D carving.

Q: The collet says 1–8 mm compatible — can I use a 6 mm bit?

Compatible is not included. The 4040-PRO and 4040-PRO MAX box lists contain only ER11 4 mm and ER11 3.175 mm, so 6 mm and 1/4 inch shanks need a separate collet. Check the collet size and the spindle's clamping range, not the word "compatible".

Q: Which of the five should I buy?

Walk the four gates. If Z is 40–60 mm and the work is thin sheet or PCBs, the 3018-PROVer V2 and Masuter Pro cover it; if you need 78 mm of travel and a larger table, the 4040-PRO and 4040-PRO MAX are the next tier; only when you need 120 mm of travel, 5000 mm/min and ±0.05 mm does the PROVerXL 4030S earn its price.
