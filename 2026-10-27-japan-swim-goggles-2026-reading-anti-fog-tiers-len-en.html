The two words that dominate an Amazon.co.jp listing — くもり止め (anti-fog) and 度付き (prescription) — each sit on top of a grading system that only Japanese manufacturers actually use, and US listings almost never explain it. Anti-fog is not one thing — it is four tiers

Laid out flat, the anti-fog claims on Amazon.co.jp fall into four very different tiers.

Tier one is a plain anti-fog coating, and the listing says nothing more than "anti-fog processing on the inner lens surface". The SWANS SR-10 Sniper page adds a warning: "こすらないようご注意ください" — do not rub it. That page is SWANS SR-10 Sniper, and it also quotes 99.99% UV cut and a 72% drag reduction versus the maker's regular models (21% versus its own racing models).

Tier two is SWANS PAF (Premium Anti-Fog), which takes the "make the coating itself last longer" route — the SRX-PAF page says about 4x the fog resistance of conventional models.

Tier three is VIEW SWIPE ANTI-FOG, which takes the "rub it back to life" route. The V540SA listing says rubbing the lens surface with your finger restores the anti-fog effect, and claims an average 10x longer anti-fog life than the maker's previous products. The women's fit V820SA runs the same system — see VIEW V820SA — and adds an orientation hint worth knowing: the direction in which the VIEW logo reads correctly is the correct up/down, because a flipped goggle leaks.

Tier four is arena re:non. The Q-CHAKU2 listing states that when the anti-fog performance starts to drop, lightly wiping the inside of the eye cup with a finger makes it last longer. That page is arena Q-CHAKU2 (WA approved).

Here is the trap: re:non is not an arena exclusive. The SWANS fitness model SW-47re carries the same wording — "PREMIUM ANTI-FOG re:non ... wipe it and it resists fogging, strong against rubbing" — on its own page at SWANS SW-47re, along with two hard numbers: the cushion contact area is about 140% of the maker's previous model, and the nose bridge ships in three sizes. Guessing an anti-fog tier from the brand name simply does not work here; you have to read the specific model page.

Reading the lens: beyond tint, check UV cut and WA approval

The conventional tint reading holds: clear for indoor pools, smoke or dark tints for brightly lit venues, mirror coatings for outdoors and triathlon. What actually differentiates listings is two other fields.

The first is the UV cut percentage, and it varies inside a single brand: SRX-PAF says 99.9% while SR-10 Sniper says 99.99%. Those are two separate published numbers, not a typo, so do not carry one figure across a whole product line.

The second is WA approval from World Aquatics. Amazon.co.jp listings routinely append "FINAから名称が変更されました" (renamed from FINA). Approved models can be used in sanctioned meets — arena Splash (WA approved) is one — whereas fitness-line pages such as arena Silky and arena Warbo only say "fitness" and never mention approval.

As for VLT (visible light transmission), Amazon.co.jp listings basically do not publish it; only some overseas retail pages do (SRCL-7N is listed at 35% VLT on some of them). I found no official VLT figure on any Amazon.co.jp page, so I am not treating it as a criterion — check the brand's own spec sheet if you need it.

There is also a structural and cost point buried in the official text: a finished goggle is "two prescription lenses plus one matching parts set" — three items, not one. On Amazon.co.jp that maps to bundles like SRCL-7M/7N two lenses + PS-SR2 parts set (S-2.00 to S-6.00), SRXCL-NPAF S-3.00 two lenses + PS-SR2 (same power in both eyes), SRXCL-MPAF S-3.00 mirrored two lenses + PS-SR2, plus the PS-SR2 parts set sold on its own.

The step-down rule has two independent sources. SWANS states it directly: objects look larger through goggles underwater, so they recommend a power slightly weaker than your everyday glasses or contacts. The retail side agrees — arena's own Hong Kong flagship listing says to "choose the goggles of 50 diopters lower than normal glasses" and gives the example of 300 degrees of myopia mapping to 250, while optical retailers commonly quote a 0.50 to 0.75 D reduction. Both line up: take your glasses power, drop about 0.50 D, then land on the nearest available step.

arena runs a different structure: AGL-4500C is a one-touch single lens, listed at arena prescription goggle lens AGL-4500C, and the page states it must be assembled with the separately sold goggle parts set AS5SGG61U, which is not in the box. Decision tree: match the goggle to your pool

IF you swim indoors for fitness and fogging is your main complaint → take a rub-to-revive tier such as SWIPE or re:non; both VIEW V540SA and SWANS SW-47re sit here.

ELSE IF you race in meets that require approved equipment → filter on WA approval first, then drag; SWANS SRX-PAF and arena Q-CHAKU2 are in this branch.

Physical limitations and hidden costs

The first hidden cost is that three items make one goggle. Buying FCL-45PAF lenses alone gets you lenses only; the listing says outright that the PS-45 parts set is required separately. If your two eyes need different powers you are buying two different lenses, not "a pair".

The physical limitation is that rub-to-revive and do-not-rub care habits are not interchangeable. SWIPE and re:non restore performance by wiping, which still consumes coating, while plain-coated models such as SR-10 Sniper explicitly say do not rub. Treating a rub-safe goggle like a plain one shortens its life; treating a plain one like a rub-safe one can end it in one session.

Wide view and low drag are mutually exclusive. The large-lens SW-47re is comfortable and puts about 140% of the previous cushion area against your face, but it has more drag than a cushionless racing model. SWANS VEGA-M re α sits in the cushioned-racing middle ground.

FAQ

Q: Can I just buy my glasses prescription in goggle form?

A: Not recommended. Objects appear magnified underwater, and both SWANS and multiple optical retailers recommend going about 0.50 D weaker than your everyday glasses, then taking the nearest available step.

Q: Can I keep rubbing a "rub to revive" coating forever?

A: Wiping still consumes coating. The V820SA page describes wetting the inner lens and stroking it gently with a wet finger, and warns against hard rubbing and dry wiping.

Q: What does WA approval actually change?

A: It is the sanctioned-meet eligibility mark from World Aquatics (Amazon.co.jp pages usually note the rename from FINA). Fitness-line listings generally do not carry it.

Q: Why no yen prices in this article?

A: My egress point is in China, so Amazon.co.jp rendered prices in a non-yen currency when I fetched these pages. Treat every figure as "as displayed at fetch time" and check the live listing.
