Rust and Python books: which edition are you buying

technical bookseditionsamazon

Two clicks in the same search box can buy you two different books. That happened to me while brushing up on Rust: two adjacent results, the same cover design, titles differing by a single ordinal. One was the 2023 second edition, the other the 2026 third edition. Opening them revealed two separate products with different page counts, different ISBNs, different on-sale dates, and companion repositories in different states of repair.

For a technical book, the edition is not metadata. It is close to being the product's other name. Change the edition and the page count, the ISBN-13, the on-sale date, the content behind the free web version and the health of the companion repo all move with it. Retail pages rarely tell you which of those five things changed. So this piece lays out the four checks I ran, and the actual differences across six books and eleven live listings.

Why one title is four products

Pull a listing apart and you find at least four independent things sharing one title, each with its own identifier system.

Edition: it decides which language or tool version the content matches. Rust 3rd maps to Rust 2024 Edition, Effective Python 3rd maps to Python 3.13. That mapping lives on the publisher page or in the preface, never in the listing title.

Format: paperback, Kindle and ebook are separate ASINs. Python Crash Course 4th is 1718505140 in paperback and B0HF41DM99 on Kindle, and the two pages do not carry the same fields at all: the Kindle page has no ISBN-10 and no print-length field.

Free web version: more and more technical books ship a full official web edition. The Rust book lives at doc.rust-lang.org/book, and Crafting Interpreters says on its own site: "Read the whole book for free. Really." The web version keeps moving; the paper is a slice of one moment.

Companion repo: the most perishable of the four. It gets archived, it stalls, and while a new edition is still pre-order it may not exist at all.

What tells you which of the four you are looking at is the ISBN-13, not the cover, the title or the price. Cover design is carried across editions, titles differ by an ordinal, and prices are converted by region and seller; in this pass the same link quoted me yuan on one route and dirhams on another. The ISBN-13 is the field that stays put and can be cross-checked.

624 minus 560 is 64 pages, or +11.4% by page count. The addition is not bulk, though. Three flaws, stated up front. The 60-second pre-order check

Step one, read three fields in the product details block: Edition, Publication date, ISBN-13. If any of the three is missing, treat the listing as incomplete, which is exactly what the Kindle edition of Python Crash Course 4th looks like: no ISBN-10, no print length.

Step two, a publication date in the future means pre-order. Add-to-cart is not the same as readable.

Step three, paste the ISBN-13 into the publisher's own search. A mismatch means same title, different product, which happens with translated editions, reprints by other publishers and third-party re-typesets.

Step four, find the companion repo on GitHub and check two things: whether archived is true, and the default branch's last commit date, not the "last updated" label on the page. This pass produced all three states: pcc_2e archived, pcc_3e stalled, pcc_4e missing.

You can skip the four steps and reuse my results, with one caveat: every field has a shelf life. This pass was scraped on November 8, 2026, so page counts and on-sale dates are as of that date.

FAQ

Q: How do I confirm which edition I am about to buy on Amazon?

A: The ordinal in the title is not enough, because Kindle and paperback titles can be identical. Use the order Edition, then ISBN-13, then Publication date. All three match, and you have the right one.

Q: Do old editions get delisted or discounted once a new one ships?

A: In all four pairs here, the old edition still has its own listing and can be added to cart, including Rust 2nd, Python Crash Course 3rd, Effective Python 2nd and DDIA 1st. The assumption that old editions vanish is wrong, which is why you can end up buying one by accident.

Q: Rust is free to read online, so is print worth it?

A: They serve different purposes. The online book currently states it is written against Rust 1.97.0 with edition 2024 and keeps changing; print is a complete slice of one moment and works offline. Use the web for always-current, print for stable page references.

Q: How different is the Kindle edition from print?

A: I only compared format and fields here (different ASIN, Kindle pages often missing ISBN-10 and print length) and did not compare page-by-page content, so I will not claim the text is identical. Before buying Kindle, use the look-inside feature to check the chapter numbering against what you need.

What to remember

Buying the wrong technical book is usually not a wrong author but a wrong edition. Under one title, the edition, the format, the free web version and the companion repo are four separate product attributes, and the listing flattens all of them into one line.

Every number here is re-checkable, and the publisher pages and APIs are named in the text. If you keep one sentence: read Edition and ISBN-13 first, then check whether Publication date is in the future, then check whether the repo is archived. Sixty seconds beats buying the wrong book twice.
