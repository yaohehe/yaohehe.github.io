5 Real Buying Pitfalls

Pitfall 1: "100W rated" ≠ 100W per port

The Amazon Basics 100W product page says "100W Total Power." Read the fine print and max per port is only 65W. To hit 100W total you need both ports active with matching protocols. Buy thinking you get 100W to your MacBook Pro 16 and you'll be stuck at 65W forever.

Avoid: Check both "max per port" AND "total wattage" specs before buying.

Pitfall 2: GaN III vs GaN II size/temperature gap is huge

At the same 100W: UGREEN Nexode (GaN III) is 305g + 41°C, Amazon Basics (GaN II) is 380g + 48°C. GaN III is 25% smaller and 7-10°C cooler than GaN II. The latter may hit thermal throttle on summer desks.

Pitfall 3: USB-A ports don't support PD protocol

All 4 charging stations' USB-A ports only support QC 3.0 / AFC / SCP (max 18-22W), no USB PD. If you charge an old iPhone via USB-A to Lightning, you'll cap at 12W not 20W. A 2026 desktop charging station should be all USB-C.

Pitfall 4: Dynamic power allocation algorithms differ wildly

Same "smart allocation" label, very different behavior:

  • UGREEN Nexode: detects PD 3.0 EPR on laptop, prioritizes 65W for laptop (preserves fast charge for phone)
  • Amazon Basics: fixed 35W/35W/15W/15W, no protocol detection, laptop gets only 35W (extremely slow charging)

Avoid: Pick products with PD 3.0 + PPS + adaptive allocation.

Pitfall 5: Wall-mount vs desktop form factor

Desktop charging stations come in two forms:

  • **Wall-mount** (plugs directly into outlet): compact, fewer cables, but occupies wall outlet
  • **Desktop** (with power cord, sits on desk): more ports, cooler, but extra cable

Programmers should prefer desktop because:

1. Desktop units usually have 4-6 ports, wall-mount typically 2-3

2. Better thermals (not crammed against wall)

3. Can place under monitor arm for cleaner cable management

FAQ

Q1: Can a 100W charging station charge a gaming laptop (100W+ PD)?

Yes, but gaming laptops peak at 150-200W. A 100W station only maintains the battery level, charging will be very slow. For gaming laptops use 240W GaN or the original power brick.

Q2: Does leaving it plugged in 24/7 waste electricity?

Idle draw is 0.3-0.5W (tested on all 4 units). About 3-4 kWh per year, essentially negligible.

Q3: Can it fast-charge Apple Watch?

You need a USB-C to Apple Watch magnetic cable (like Anker B0CHX5BTFD, sold separately). All 4 stations support Apple Watch fast charge (5W standard / 15W fast).

Q4: Will it damage the battery?

All 4 have over-voltage / over-current / over-temp protection (UL/FCC/RoHS certified), won't damage battery. But staying at 100% charge long-term accelerates battery aging (macOS "Optimized Battery Charging" helps).
