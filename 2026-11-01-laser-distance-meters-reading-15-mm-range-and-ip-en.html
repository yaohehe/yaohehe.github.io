How to Read Laser Distance Meter Specs: ±1.5 mm, Range

laser distance meterbuying guidespecifications

Shopping for a laser distance meter, the two numbers on the listing are usually "±1.5 mm" and "range 120 m". Neither is false, but each carries a set of conditions — and those conditions tend to live in the footnotes of the user manual, not on the product page.

I actually went through Leica's official user manual and official product page, Bosch's official operating manual and official product page, DeWalt's official product page, and the current status of ISO 16331-1. There is no hands-on measurement in this article. Every figure comes from a manufacturer document or the standard itself. Wherever I did the arithmetic myself, it is labelled explicitly as a linear extrapolation from an official footnote.

Three words that get collapsed into one line: accuracy, range, resolution

Product pages tend to print these together, but they describe different things.

Accuracy is how far the reading may sit from the true value — and it always comes with conditions. Range is the longest distance at which the return signal is still usable, again under stated conditions. Resolution is the smallest step the display can show: Leica lists 0.1 mm for the DISTO D2G, Bosch's manual lists 1/32 in (0.8 mm) for the GLM100-23. Resolution has nothing to do with accuracy.

Collapse the three and the usual result is paying more for a tool with a finer last digit and a looser tolerance.

  • Needs a visible dot in bright interiors: Leica DISTO D2G, 518 nm green — but the official table shows shorter range and shorter battery life than the red-beam D2. Listing: Leica DISTO D2G.

Number one: ±1.5 mm holds from 0.05 to 10 m, and no further

This is the column most easily skipped and most easily bought wrong.

Leica's official user manual gives 1.5 mm under favourable conditions and 3 mm under unfavourable conditions. The real limits sit in the footnote: tolerances apply from 0.05 m to 10 m at a 95% confidence level; above 10 m the deviation may degrade by 0.10 mm/m (and by 0.2 mm/m above 100 m); under unfavourable conditions it may degrade by 0.15 mm/m above 10 m.

Extrapolating linearly from that official footnote — this is extrapolation, not a full-range tolerance published by the manufacturer — a tool rated ±1.5 mm works out like this:

DistanceFavourable (1.5 + 0.10×(d−10))Unfavourable (3.0 + 0.15×(d−10))
10 m1.5 mm3.0 mm
30 m3.5 mm6.0 mm
50 m5.5 mm9.0 mm
100 mabout 10.5 mm (0.2 mm/m past 100 m)about 16.5 mm

At 50 m, that "±1.5 mm" can widen by roughly 3.7x per the official footnote. That is not a manufacturer cutting corners; the headline number simply never covered past 10 m.

Leica's own product page labels the source: the row reads "Typ. distance measuring accuracy (ISO 16331-1) ± 1.5 mm". ISO 16331-1:2017 (2nd edition, published 2017-03, confirmed current on 2023-01-25 per the ISO committee page) specifies the laboratory procedure for handheld laser distance meters: favourable conditions are roughly 3 klux ambient light against a white painted wall at room temperature, unfavourable conditions are 30 klux of background light across the full declared operating temperature range. Leica's footnote defines favourable conditions the same way — a white, diffusely reflecting target, weak background light, moderate temperature.

Bosch uses different formatting but the same structure. The GLM100-23 manual states two rows outright:

  • Favourable: ±1/16 in (±1.5 mm)
  • Unfavourable: ±5/32 in (±3.0 mm)

Footnote A adds three more preconditions: measured from the back edge of the tool, a high-reflectivity target such as a white painted wall, weak background light at 68 °F (20 °C) — and a distance-dependent deviation of ±0.05 mm/m must be factored in on top.

One more trap: Bosch prints imperial and metric side by side, and they are not a conversion. 1/16 in is really 1.5875 mm but the sheet says 1.5 mm; 5/32 in is 3.97 mm but the sheet says 3.0 mm. These are two independently stated figures — do not derive the metric column from the imperial one.

Number two: range — 150 m favourable, 80 m unfavourable

Range is the number most likely to be read as a single value, and in the official manual it is also two columns.

Leica's manual gives these for the DISTO D2 and D2G:

ModelFavourableUnfavourableReduction
DISTO D2 (red 635 nm)0.05–150 m0.05–80 m46.7%
DISTO D2G (green 518 nm)0.05–120 m0.05–70 m41.7%

Under ISO 16331-1 the cause is explicit: unfavourable conditions raise background illumination from roughly 3 klux to 30 klux and require the whole declared temperature span. The standard also specifies a target plate of (95±5)% reflectance measuring 0.25 m × 0.25 m, tested at +5 °C / +20 °C / +40 °C. Lower target reflectance and stronger background light both degrade the signal-to-noise ratio, and the range drops with it.

So "this one reaches 150 m" and "I can reach 150 m on a sunny jobsite" are two different claims.

Number three: what the green 518 nm beam costs, printed in the official table

Green really is more visible — Leica's product page states the DISTO D2G dot is about four times as visible as a red one, and Bosch's page says the GLM165-25G beam is "up to 4X brighter than standard red beams".

But put the two siblings from the same maker next to each other and the cost is in the official table too. The DISTO D2 and D2G share a weight of 116 g, dimensions of 127 × 50.5 × 24.5 mm, an IP54 rating and ±1.5 mm accuracy. Only these rows differ:

Green is bought with a fifth of the range and roughly three tenths of the runtime, and Leica attaches the same note to both rows: "Reduced when used with Bluetooth®". That is a trade-off, not a defect — but listings usually lead with the 4x visibility and leave these two columns out.

Number four: the spot diameter — 60 mm at 100 m, not 6 mm

The laser dot is not a geometric point; it grows linearly with distance. Leica's manual gives the pairing directly: 10 m / 50 m / 100 m correspond to a spot diameter of 6 mm / 30 mm / 60 mm, six millimetres more per ten metres.

At 100 m you are aiming a 60 mm circle, wider than plenty of window frames and trims. Corners are handled with the end-piece, not with eyesight — Leica lists a "Multifunctional end-piece" with "Automatic reference detection for end-piece", meaning the flipped-out piece adds its own offset automatically.

Comparing this column across brands needs care: Bosch's manual gives a divergence limit of <1.5 mrad (full angle), which works out to no more than about 150 mm at 100 m, while Leica states 60 mm at the same distance. One is a measured value and the other is an upper bound, so the two columns cannot be compared directly — the same problem this site ran into with ripple bandwidth on thermal cameras.

Number five: IP rating, laser class, and the "indoor only" line

IP rating and laser class govern different things, but listings tend to merge them into one bullet.

Laser class is uniform here: Class 2, 635 nm or 518 nm, <1 mW, as written in both the Leica and Bosch manuals. This column carries almost no differentiating information.

IP rating varies a lot, and you have to read the official page rather than the listing copy:

  • Bosch GLM100-23: the official product page lists IP Rating: None, and the manual's "Intended Use" section states the tool "is suitable for measuring only indoors".
  • Bosch GLM165-25G: officially IP65.
  • DeWalt DW099S: officially IP54.
  • Leica DISTO D1 / D2 / D2G: all officially IP54, described in the manual as dust- and splash-water protected.

One official contradiction worth noting: the GLM100-23 product page lists an operating temperature of −10 °C to 40 °C, while the manual says −10 °C to +45 °C. Same manufacturer, two documents; the manual is the safer one to buy against.

Decision table: work backwards from what you measure

  • Indoor dimensions under 10 m (furniture, room widths, ceiling heights): every tool here is accurate enough; a small body such as the GLM100-23 or DISTO D1 is the practical pick.
  • 30–50 m, target not necessarily a white wall: stop reading the ±1.5 mm and go to the distance-dependent deviation footnote (Leica 0.10 mm/m, Bosch 0.05 mm/m), then estimate from the unfavourable column.
  • Direct sun or a jobsite: look at IP and the unfavourable range. The GLM165-25G's IP65 and 165 ft fit better; the GLM100-23 is explicitly limited to indoors.
  • Need data on a phone or computer: the DISTO D2G (Bluetooth 6.0 plus NFC) and the DW099S (Tool Connect) both have an official path, at the cost of battery life.
  • Stakeout, Pythagoras, area and volume: the GLM165-25G offers 11 functions, but stakeout accuracy is a separate row — do not infer it from the length accuracy.

FAQ

Q: Does ISO 16331-1 define accuracy classes A/B/C?

A: No. The ISO committee page summarises ISO 16331-1:2017 as specifying procedures for checking compliance of handheld laser distance meters with their performance specifications — a test method, not a tolerance ladder. The "accuracy classes" circulating online are not defined by this standard.

Q: Is a green beam always better than red?

A: Not always. Green is more visible in bright conditions (about 4x per the manufacturers), but Leica's own table shows the green D2G trailing the red D2 on range (120 m vs 150 m), measurements per battery set (5,000 vs 7,000) and runtime (10 h vs 14 h).

Q: Why can't I reach the rated range in sunlight?

A: Because the rated range is usually the favourable-condition figure. ISO 16331-1 raises background illumination to roughly 30 klux for unfavourable conditions; Leica's manual drops the range from 150 m to 80 m (D2) and from 120 m to 70 m (D2G) accordingly.

Q: Is Class 2 safe?

A: Every official document here lists Class 2 at <1 mW (Leica and Bosch manuals both). Class 2 is a low-power visible-light category, but still do not view the beam with optical instruments or point it at eyes or strongly reflective surfaces.

Q: Does a tighter stated accuracy always mean a more accurate tool?

A: Only within the distance band the figure covers. Leica's footnote says ±1.5 mm applies from 0.05 to 10 m at 95% confidence and degrades by 0.10 mm/m beyond that, while the DISTO D1's ±2.0 mm covers its whole 40 m range. The two figures use different scopes and cannot be compared directly — I could not retrieve an official distance-term footnote for the D1, so that is as far as the published documents take it.

Q: Does leaving Bluetooth on affect anything?

A: Leica attaches the same note to both the measurements-per-set and the service-life rows: "Reduced when used with Bluetooth®". Official Bluetooth range is <10 m.
