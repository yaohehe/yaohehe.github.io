The shop prints two numbers; the spec page prints none

Start with the two number formats, because everything below depends on which one you are reading.

Every sheet on butterfly.shop follows the same pattern: a bare number, then the tax-included figure in brackets. Rozena reads "5,000 yen (tax included 5,500)"; Glazer reads "5,500 yen (tax included 6,050)". The first number is the tax-excluded body price, and the bracket is what you actually pay.

The brand site, butterfly.co.jp, writes it the other way: Rozena's price row says "5,500 yen (tax included)", Glazer says "6,050 yen (tax included)". The same sheet is 5,000 on one page and 5,500 on the other. The 500 yen gap is the tax.

I divided all 44 listings out: every single one equals the tax-excluded price times 1.10, no exceptions. So the tax-included reading is something you can check yourself rather than memorise: see a tax-excluded figure, multiply by 1.1; see a tax-included figure, divide by 1.1 to recover the body price the manufacturer is working from.

The more consequential detail sits halfway down the spec page. For Tenergy, Dignics and Zyre, the price row does not contain a number at all. It says "open price". The footnote at the bottom of the page reads: listed prices are manufacturer's suggested retail prices, excluding open-price items.

That footnote matters if you are buying from abroad: for the flagship lines, an official retail price does not exist. The only anchor you can compare against is the tax-excluded figure Butterfly's own shop publishes. That is why every number below uses tax-excluded pricing as the common unit.

The ¥5,500 tacky sheet out-spins the ¥9,460 flagship

Here is the comparison that stings.

Glazer 09C is a tacky high-tension inverted sheet: official hardness 42, speed 75, spin 87, arc 95, ¥5,500 tax-excluded (¥6,050 with tax). Tenergy 05 is a high-tension inverted sheet: hardness 36, speed 83, spin 76, arc 79, ¥8,600 tax-excluded (¥9,460 with tax).

The sheet that costs ¥3,100 less is rated higher on both hardness and spin. The expensive one wins on speed, 83 against 75.

Nobody mislabelled anything. These are two different design directions. Tacky topsheets grip the ball, trading speed for spin; Tenergy relies on sponge elasticity to launch it, trading arc height for speed. The extra ¥3,100 does not buy stronger numbers, it buys a different way of sending the ball out.

If your game is close-to-table counterdriving, Glazer 09C's speed rating of 75 will cost you. If you are coming from a Chinese tacky sheet, the ¥5,500 tier is the cheaper entry. That tier holds two sheets, Glazer (38 hardness, 81 speed, 73 spin) and Glazer 09C, both ¥5,500.

The ¥10,000 customs line sits between 9,500 and 10,450

In the cost structure of importing sheets, one thing is easier to get wrong than the price gap: shipments for personal use are exempt from duty and consumption tax when the dutiable value is below ¥10,000, and taxable above it.

Run the two official numbers against that line. A single ¥8,600 sheet is ¥9,460 with tax — below the line on either reading. Two sheets are ¥17,200 or ¥18,920 — above it on either reading. So the ¥8,600 tier reaches the same conclusion regardless of which number you use: one is exempt, two are not.

The ¥9,500 tier is the one place where the reading flips the answer. Tax-excluded it is ¥9,500, below the line; tax-included it is ¥10,450, above it. On the same single sheet, the two numbers point opposite ways.

The boundary has to be stated: dutiable value is not the same as the sticker price — it includes shipping and insurance, and the conversion is decided by customs, so this article does not offer a formula. The exemption for personal-import parcels is also scheduled to end on 1 April 2028, according to the reform direction published by customs and the Ministry of Finance, so the line itself has an expiry date.

The ordering rule therefore gets simpler: do not stuff two sheets from the same tier into one shipment to save on postage, because that is exactly how you cross the line. One flagship sheet, or two entry sheets, is safer than three mixed.

Four checks before you order

First, read the tax-excluded number before the tax-included one. Tax-excluded times 1.10 is what you pay, and that 10 percent changes the answer near the ¥10,000 line.

Second, go by item number rather than marketing name. Amazon titles carry the item number (06030, 06050, 06140 — five digits), and the official spec pages use the same code. A family with 05, 05 FX and 05 Hard in it is easy to misread by name and hard to misread by code.

Third, substitute inside the tier when something is out of stock. With both ¥5,500 sheets gone, stepping down to Rozena at ¥5,000 makes more sense than stepping up to ¥8,600, because the step up buys a different product line rather than a harder version of the same sheet.

Fourth, treat prices as captured-at-time values. Amazon pricing follows sellers and this machine still sees a non-yen currency; the official shop's tax-excluded prices are steadier, but stock badges move. Check both again on the day you order.

FAQ

Q: Which sheet should I pick inside the ¥8,600 tier?

Decide whether you want speed or spin first. On the official ratings Tenergy 80 at speed 85 is the fastest in the tier, Tenergy 05 Hard at spin 96 is the spinniest, and Tenergy 05 FX at hardness 32 is the softest. All three cost ¥8,600; you are choosing a direction, not a rank.

Q: Does a harder sheet cost more?

Not within one brand. Roundell at hardness 35 sells for ¥4,200 while Tenergy 05 at 36 sells for ¥8,600; Glazer 09C at hardness 42 sells for ¥5,500, which is ¥3,100 less than Tenergy 05 at 36. Price follows the product line, not the hardness number.

Q: If Amazon has no listing for a sheet, is it discontinued?

Check the official catalogue first. Discontinuation is about whether the item number is still on the spec page, not about whether a shop has stock. When I checked, Dignics 05 was sold out on the official shop and existed on Amazon only as bundles, yet its spec page was intact: item 06040, released 1 April 2019.

Q: Can I compare Amazon's displayed price directly with the official price?

Not by dividing one by the other. Amazon is showing a non-yen currency this round (TWD) with a conversion layer and seller pricing in between, so what transfers is relative order, not a multiplier. For absolute figures, compare only Butterfly's own tax-excluded and tax-included pair.

Closing

The most useful thing about this price table is that it admits it is not ordered by performance. Ten sheets share ¥8,600 with 11 points of hardness between them; a ¥5,500 tacky sheet out-spins the flagship; and the two +56 percent steps cut across technical and product lines rather than specifications.

So the buying order should be reversed: set the tax-excluded budget per sheet to fix the tier, pick a direction inside that tier, and only then check stock. Get the tier wrong and every later choice is paying for something you did not need.
