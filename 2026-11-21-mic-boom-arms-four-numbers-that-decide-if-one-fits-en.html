Clamp thickness: the number that sends arms back

Clamp thickness is the easiest number to skip and the easiest to return a product over. The listings are far apart: PSA1+ clamps to desks up to 70 mm, Gator's low-profile arm says 2.17 in (55 mm), InnoGear says 2 in (about 51 mm) and explicitly requires a flat, unsloped edge. Both Elgato arms emphasise a padded clamp for tight spaces in their bullet points but do not put a millimetre limit there, so check the specification table before ordering.

The fix takes a ruler. Measure the desk exactly where the clamp will sit, not the leg or a decorative trim strip. The most common return reason I see is a desk that turns out to be too thick to grip, and a rounded edge is worse than the number suggests: a clamp needs a flat surface, and a rounded lip can leave it one millimetre short of biting.

Load rating: weigh the mic and the shock mount together

The second number is load, and the unit is the microphone plus its shock mount, not the bare mic. RØDE's own PSA1+ page says it works with microphones or shock mounts from 94 g to 1.2 kg and names the PodMic, Procaster and Broadcaster; the listing for the same arm says 0.25 kg to 1.2 kg. Both texts were still live when I checked. This is less a contradiction than two baselines: the brand counts the light NT-USB Mini, the listing counts a typical dynamic mic. If yours weighs less than 250 g, the brand page is the safer reference.

Shure SH-BROADCAST2 puts 4.4 lb (about 2 kg) in the product title, the highest stated figure in this group; InnoGear claims 3.3 lb (1.5 kg). If you use a heavy microphone such as the SM7B, my earlier dynamic microphone XLR and USB guide lists body weights you can add before comparing them with these ratings.

An overloaded arm rarely drops the mic. It drifts downward slowly, moving the capsule away from your mouth and shrinking your level over a take. That gets misdiagnosed as a loose spring when it is really a weight problem.

Reach and posture: low profile is not always right

The third number is reach, but the real choice is posture. Elgato's Wave Mic Arm LP lists 740 mm (29.1 in) of horizontal reach, all-metal anti-torsion construction and magnetic covers over the cable channels, with the arm sitting below shoulder level so it stays out of frame. Gator states 33 in (84 cm) extension and 90 degrees of vertical movement; Shure states 33 in reach, 360 degrees horizontal and 90 degrees vertical.

The newer Wave Arm MK.2 says something different: its riser lets the mic rise above a tall monitor or stay low, so a low-profile arm can be the wrong answer if you shoot in front of a large upright display. Framing is not only about the arm, either, and my video-call lighting guide breaks down what else blocks a shot.

Threads and cable routing: two free fixes

The fourth number is the thread. The US 5/8 in-27 and European 3/8 in-16 standards are different, and losing the adapter means waiting for a replacement. PSA1+ ships both thread adapters; Gator states compatibility with 5/8-27 and 3/8-16; Elgato's Wave Arm LP ships 1/4-to-3/8 and 1/4-to-5/8 adapters and lists all three sizes in its thread field; Shure's specification table lists 5/8 in only, so a European microphone needs a separate ring.

Cable routing decides whether your desk looks tidy and whether a joint eventually pinches the cable. PSA1+, both Elgato arms and Gator use covered channels with magnetic lids; InnoGear uses an exposed spring arm where you tie the cable on yourself. An XLR cable is stiffer than USB, so a closed channel earns its keep. My dynamic versus condenser comparison also covers how much wiring each path needs.

Troubleshooting: what actually goes wrong

  • The clamp cannot bite and the desk wobbles: measure the desk where the clamp touches it and compare with the 70 / 55 / 51 mm tiers. A rounded edge slips even at the stated thickness; use a flat area or a desk-mounting plate
  • The arm drifts down and the mic leaves your mouth: mic plus shock mount exceeds the rating. PSA1+ is 94 g-1.2 kg on the brand page but starts at 0.25 kg on the listing, so use your own lightest device as the baseline; heavy microphones belong on the 4.4 lb tier
  • The thread will not screw in: 5/8-27 and 3/8-16 are different standards, and Elgato adds 1/4 adapters. Check the direction of the supplied adapter, male-to-female or the reverse, rather than trusting "fits most microphones"
  • An XLR cable gets pinched at a joint: covered magnetic channels avoid it; on exposed spring arms, leave slack along the arm and never tension the moving end
  • You hear creaks during a take: cheap springs and knobs are the usual source. Spring damping is a specific claim on the PSA1+ listing, not a synonym for every "silent" badge

FAQ

Q: My desk is 60 mm thick. Which of these fit?

The PSA1+ listing says 70 mm, so it fits; Gator at 55 mm and InnoGear at roughly 51 mm do not. Neither Elgato arm states a limit in its bullet points, so check the specification table first. PSA1+ also ships a threaded desk mount, which bypasses the clamp altogether if you can drill the desk.

Q: Which arm handles a heavy microphone like the SM7B?

On paper the Shure SH-BROADCAST2 at 4.4 lb (about 2 kg) is highest, followed by InnoGear at 1.5 kg and PSA1+ at a 1.2 kg brand limit. Add the shock mount weight to the microphone before comparing. The MK.2 listing gives no capacity figure, so do not judge it by appearance.

Q: Should I buy a 5/8-27 or a 3/8-16 thread?

Neither, until you look at your microphone or shock mount: those are US and European standards. RØDE and Gator supply both adapters, Elgato supplies 1/4-based adapters, and Shure lists 5/8 only. Adapters are cheap, but a missing one stops a recording session.

Q: Low profile or riser?

For a camera-facing stream, a tight desk and a short monitor, pick low profile: Wave LP, Gator or Shure. For an upright monitor or a mic coming down from above, pick a posture-flexible arm such as the MK.2. The deciding question is what blocks your frame, not which one costs less.

Last word

A microphone boom arm is not a "clip it anywhere" accessory: clamp thickness decides whether it fits, load rating decides whether it slowly droops, reach and posture decide whether it blocks the shot, and the thread decides whether you can record on day one. All four figures are on a listing or a brand page, and ten minutes of checking before ordering beats a return afterwards.
