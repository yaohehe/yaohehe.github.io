"Japan exclusive" is too blunt a label to be useful. I checked 29 live Amazon.co.jp listings across Yonex, Mizuno and Asics and read the three brands' own press releases, and what I found was not "Japan has it, the US does not". If you cannot find a model by name on a US site, the usual reason is not that it is Japan-only. It is that the name you searched was never the name used in that market.

Three launch rhythms, and nothing else explains the mismatch

Treating "available in Japan, not available in the US" as one phenomenon never explains anything. Split it into three rhythms and each one gets a test you can actually run.

Rhythm one: global simultaneous. Yonex's SUBAXIA GT is the clean example. The Japanese press release on yonex.co.jp states "2026年1月28日より世界同時発売" — simultaneous worldwide release on 28 January 2026. The US site us.yonex.com and the Chinese site yonex.cn published substantively the same copy with the same date. For models in this bucket, buying from Japan buys you the same shoe you would get anywhere; Japan's advantage is limited to colourways, last widths and the size run.

Rhythm two: Japan first, overseas unconfirmed. Mizuno's SKY PRIME sits here. Japan got a general release on 11 September 2026 at ¥24,200 including tax. A running-shoe database recording the model against Mizuno Japan's own figures states plainly that overseas launch timing and overseas MSRP remain unconfirmed. One discrepancy worth flagging: traditional-Chinese running media reported ¥24,900 at the same time, against the official ¥24,200, and I am using the official figure while recording the conflict here. Mizuno's Taiwan site also lists SKY PRIME, but under a different SKU number, J1GW265071, priced in New Taiwan Dollars. Same generation, two markets, and nowhere does an official page put the two codes and two prices side by side for you.

Rhythm three: the ranges don't sync and the marketing name isn't portable. The Yonex 65 line is the clearest case. Amazon.co.jp listing titles only ever read "POWER CUSHION 65Z", "65Z VA", "65Z Wide" or "65Z VA Slim", and some titles carry the product code inline (SHBVAZM, SHBVAZW). The "65 Z4" style generation label that circulates in English and Chinese discussions did not appear in a single listing title when I searched for it on Amazon.co.jp. That is not evidence the generation does not exist. It is evidence that the Japanese retail naming scheme does not carry that generation information, so matching on it is structurally doomed.

The product code is the only thing that matches across markets

Put the conclusion up front: do not match across markets on the marketing name — match on the product code. Marketing names are written for shoppers and get rewritten per market. Product codes are written for supply chains and retail systems, so they change far less often.

Asics makes this easiest to check. The Japanese release says GEL-KAYANO 33 ships as "4 product codes" priced uniformly at ¥22,000 including tax, using Japanese pricing conventions. Asics' Taiwan site carries the same generation — GEL-KAYANO 33 PLATINUM women's — under a bare numeric code, 1012C066-020, priced in NT$. Neither official page puts the other's price next to its own. So the question "is Japan cheaper" is one no brand answers for you; you do the conversion or you don't get an answer.

Yonex works the same way. The men's SUBAXIA GT carries the code SHBSG1M (a Yonex item code reproduced in retailer spec sheets, sizes 22.0–30.0 plus 31.0 and 32.0 cm, made in Vietnam), while the Amazon.co.jp title carries only the katakana spelling and no code at all. If you want to confirm you are ordering from the global release batch, comparing titles will not tell you — checking SHBSG1M will.

Decision tree: when importing from Amazon.co.jp is worth it

  • IF the Japanese release carries the phrase "世界同時発売" (simultaneous worldwide release) → this model does not need importing. Compare colourways, last widths and return friction instead. SUBAXIA GT is this case.
  • ELSE IF you can find the same product code on an overseas site, not the same name → it is the same production batch. Compare landed cost, using codes like SHBSG1M or SHBVAZM as your key.
  • ELSE IF overseas sites only show a marketing name with no matching code → it is likely a Japan-first or domestic-only SKU. Go to cost math: Japanese official price plus international shipping, forwarding fees and possible duties, and accept that after-sales runs through Japan.
  • ELSE what you actually want is a small size or an unusual last (22.0 cm upwards, wide or slim grading) → Japanese supply density genuinely beats most overseas stores here, and this is the least contested reason to shop the Japanese line.
  • Fallback: if none of the three resolves it, don't buy. In model-line matters the cost of guessing wrong is one pair of shoes you cannot wear and cannot easily return.

Landed cost: Japan Customs' threshold and two traps

Japan Customs states its own rule plainly: goods with a total customs value of ¥10,000 or less are exempt from customs duty and consumption tax (Japan Customs, English Q&A document FAX1006e). For personal imports the customs value is calculated as 60% of the overseas retail price — that 0.6 factor appears repeatedly in Customs' own worked examples and in the personal-import notes published by Japanese marketplaces. Run backwards, the roughly ¥16,666 duty-free ceiling quoted around the web falls straight out of this arithmetic.

The first trap: part of footwear is excluded from that relief. The same Customs document names categories that do not qualify, among them leather shoes and footwear with leather soles. Synthetic-upper running shoes and badminton shoes on rubber outsoles generally sit outside that definition, but if you are buying a leather pair, do not assume the exemption applies — budget for full assessment.

The second trap: this relief has an expiry date. Japan's Ministry of Finance published a partial amendment to the Customs Tariff Act that removes the duty and consumption-tax exemption for small personal-import parcels with effect from 1 April 2028. The ¥10,000 line is a rule with a sunset attached, not a constant you can build long-run price comparisons on.

On currency, public rates hovered around US$1 to ¥157.9 in late October 2026, roughly $0.00635 per yen the other way, with about 1% of movement across a 30-day window. At a ¥20,000 price point that swing moves the landed cost by a few hundred yen — normally less than the spread between shipping options — so settle freight before you optimise for exchange rate.

FAQ

Q: How do I pick a size for Yonex badminton shoes on Amazon.co.jp?

A: Japanese listings size in centimetres, and the Yonex line runs from 22.0 cm in half-centimetre steps. Use the Japanese centimetre size you already wear; do not reverse-engineer it from a US size.

Q: Can I find "65 Z4" on Amazon.co.jp?

A: Not by that string — I searched and no returned listing title contained it. Japanese retail titles use "65Z", "65Z VA", "65Z Wide" and "65Z VA Slim", some with codes SHBVAZM or SHBVAZW in the title. To pin down the generation, check the brand's official pages.

Q: Will a ¥20,000 pair of running shoes get taxed on import to Japan?

A: Under the Customs rule the taxable value is 60% of the overseas retail price, and ¥10,000 or less is exempt from duty and consumption tax. A ¥20,000 pair lands near ¥12,000 of taxable value, above the line, so category duty plus 10% consumption tax applies, with extra exclusions for leather footwear. Customs sets the final figure at clearance.

Q: Why doesn't the model name I found on an English site match the Japanese material?

A: Because marketing names get rewritten per market and product codes don't. Match on the code printed in official releases — SHBSG1M for the SUBAXIA GT, SHBVAZM and SHBVAZW for the 65Z VA — rather than on the name.
