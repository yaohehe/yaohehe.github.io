I actually went through Arena's Japan direct store and the Amazon.co.jp listings for the same COBRA EDGE goggles line by line, and the first thing worth reporting is this: the official store prices COBRA EDGE at ¥16,500 tax included, while Amazon.co.jp carries two pages that both say コブラエッジ and showed EUR 72.71 and EUR 146.56 on the days I fetched them. One pair of goggles, three prices, and the widest gap is exactly double.

That is not even the awkward part. Competitive swim goggles in Japan get described by three numbering systems at once: the store uses a 商品番号 starting with AR454, the brand uses a ブランド商品番号 starting with AS5 or AS6, and Amazon.co.jp drops the latter into a field labelled 型番. All three point at the same object, but misread one and you will end up parked on a page you should not order from.

TL;DR: if you just want something for lane swimming, the bottom of the ladder is the ¥2,530 シルキー, but its page title never says 公式大会可 — I come back to that. If you train and race twice a year, the ¥5,390 COBRA CORE covers it; the ladder below walks each step. If you race, buy the model coded AS5FGG00U — official price ¥16,500, and the matching Amazon page is arena COBRA EDGE レーシングゴーグル.

Two listings, one model, prices a factor of two apart

The two コブラエッジ pages on Amazon.co.jp are built the same way and priced nearly double apart. The fastest way to tell them apart is not the main image and not the title. It is the 型番 field, a few rows down in the product information table.

The first page lists 型番 as AS5FGG00U, with GTIN 04571602284298, unit count 1, and warranty なし. That AS5FGG00U string matches the ブランド商品番号 on Arena's own store page for COBRA EDGE exactly, which is how you know it is the official model sold through a different channel. It showed EUR 72.71, which at the 2026-11-16 ECB reference rate of 1 EUR = 175.78 JPY works out to roughly ¥12,780, about twenty percent under the official tax-included price.

The second page is コブラエッジ スワイプ 曇り止め レーシング水泳ゴーグル, and its 型番 reads 006871_120_TU. That string starts with neither AS5 nor AS6, nor AGL nor ARN, and it does not match any Arena numbering scheme at all — it is the seller's own stock code. The same page's title carries both ノンミラーレンズ and ポリカーボネートミラー/非ミラーレンズ, two mutually exclusive lens descriptions, and it showed EUR 146.56. I did not order from it and I would not use it as a price benchmark.

So the rule is one sentence long: open the 型番 field and check whether it starts with AS5, AS6, AGL or ARN. If it does, it is the brand's own model number. If it does not, it is a seller-generated code, and you have to confirm specs and colourway somewhere else on the page. The rule holds across the Arena catalogue, not just goggles.

型番 is not 商品番号: AR454 and AS5 are two separate ledgers

On the store page, COBRA EDGE carries 商品番号 AR454DU014723 and ブランド商品番号 AS5FGG00U. Two fields, one table, easy to read as two spellings of one thing. They serve different purposes.

The AR454 series is a distribution code. The same model in a different colourway or configuration gets a different number. Take COBRA ULTRA: the mirror-only version is AR454DU017105 (AS5SGG02U), the mirror-plus-Swipe version is AR454DU014101 (AS5SGG01U). Different 商品番号, different brand code. The AS5 and AS6 series, by contrast, is the brand model number, and it is what the official site, the Amazon 型番 field and the packaging all use.

Read the string and it decomposes. AS5 versus AS6 is the season: AS5 covers the 2025 autumn/winter release, AS6 the 2026 items. The two letters after that are the category — GG for goggles, SC for caps, WM for training swimwear. The next two digits are the sequence within that season and category, and the final letter is the fit: U unisex, M men, L women, J junior. All three junior goggles on the store end in J, and the Amazon titles carry the age bands 6歳～12歳 and 3歳～8歳.

With that in hand, AR454DU014723 stops being noise: store code, then AS5FGG00U, then 2025 season, goggles, item 00, unisex. Buy by the AS5FGG00U part and it does not matter which seller's stock code wraps around it.

公式大会可 marks World Aquatics approval, not quality

Every racing goggle on the store carries a short label after its name, most often 公式大会可. The meaning is pinned down in the COBRA EDGE copy itself: 「公式大会に出場するために必要なWORLD AQUATICS（世界水泳連盟）承認モデル」.

So the label answers "can I wear this in a sanctioned race", not "is this good". It maps to a binary: World Aquatics approval either exists or it does not.

The contrast is clean. COBRA EDGE, COBRA ULTRA, COBRA CORE, Aquaforce Swift, Q-CHAKU2 and トレンティ ジュニア all carry 公式大会可 in the page title. The ¥2,530 シルキー does not — its title reads 【シルキー】フィットネスゴーグル（リノンくもり止め）, a fitness line, not a racing line.

These are not the same thing. The first governs whether you can wear it in the water during a race. The second governs whether you can carry it into the call room beforehand. What each meet actually enforces in the call room remains a matter for that meet's own entry requirements, so check the official page for the event you are entering. The brand label is the brand's self-declaration, not the rulebook.

Where the ¥10,000 duty-free line cuts the ladder

Japanese customs treats personal imports this way: a dutiable value at or below ¥10,000 is exempt from duty and consumption tax; above it, the dutiable value is multiplied by 60 percent to form the assessment base, then duty and 10 percent consumption tax stack on top. On this particular ladder the line falls in an awkward spot.

At official tax-included prices, exactly one item crosses ¥10,000: COBRA EDGE at ¥16,500. The next most expensive, COBRA ULTRA mirror plus Swipe at ¥6,490, stays under it, and so does everything below.

In other words the landed cost of this goggle is not "2.5 times more", it is "2.5 times more and now taxable". Converted at the Amazon page's own displayed price, the same model falls back under the line — which is the practical reason to spend two minutes on the 型番 field.

Two caveats belong with that number. Leather goods and some apparel do not qualify for the exemption. And Japan's finance ministry has set 2028-04-01 as the date the personal-import de minimis exemption ends. Swim goggles are neither leather nor footwear, so they sit inside the line for now, but the line itself has an expiry date.

FAQ

Q: Two Amazon.co.jp pages both say コブラエッジ — which one do I buy?

The one whose 型番 is AS5FGG00U. The other lists 006871_120_TU, a seller stock code, carries contradictory lens descriptions in its title, and is priced at twice the first.

Q: COBRA EDGE costs ¥10,010 more than COBRA ULTRA. What is the difference?

Per Arena's own copy: it inherits the ULTRA side bar, then widens the field of view up and sideways and thins the lens edge. Both are World Aquatics approved models with identical approval status, so the money buys fit and field-of-view margin, not eligibility.

Q: I only swim laps, do I need the 公式大会可 label?

No. The ¥2,530 シルキー is a fitness line and is fine for lane swimming. The reverse matters more: if you race even once a year, buy from the models carrying the label, because there is no after-the-fact fix for that.

Q: Can the ¥1,540 mesh cap go into the call room?

Depends on the part number. AS6FSC56U and AS6FSC57U are marked 招集所持ち込み可; AS6FSCA4U and AS6FSC53U are marked 招集所持ち込み不可, at the same price. The difference is the design line, not the price. Check the specific meet's requirements as well.

Q: How many pieces do I need for a prescription setup?

Two. The lens AS5SGG60U at ¥1,760 and the parts set AS5SGG61U at ¥880 are separate part numbers, and the official parts title states it is for AGL4500C and AS5SGG60U.
