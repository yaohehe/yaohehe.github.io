Start with the conclusion. If your working day moves between annotating code, sketching architecture, and marking up PDFs, what a pen tablet really changes is not your drawing precision. It changes how your hand and your eyes split the work. A screenless pad makes you look up at the monitor and down at the pad. A pen display puts the surface you draw on inside the screen. The first is cheaper, tougher, and harder to break. The second costs more and takes desk space, but keeps hand and eye together. Neither is better. They suit different work.

The primary variable: which glass your hand looks at

A screenless pen tablet is a standalone sensor pad used with a separate pen. When you draw, you look down at the pad and up at your monitor. A pen display is itself a monitor: the cursor lands wherever the nib lands.

That difference matters more than any line in a spec sheet. A screenless pad splits drawing and looking into two separate actions, and looking down for hours gets your neck into an argument. A pen display merges them, at the cost of the screen itself: desk space and money. Most of the price gap between comparable models is essentially the cost of that glass and its backlight.

If your workflow is mainly flowcharts, architecture diagrams, or PDF markup, a screenless pad is enough, because the result is already on the screen you are looking at. If your work needs large empty areas and precise shading, the parallax between nib and cursor starts to matter, and a screenless pad does not have that problem at all.

The secondary variable: EMR versus AES, which decides charging

Wacom's technology pages explain this most clearly. Under electromagnetic resonance (EMR), sensors beneath the display generate an electromagnetic field, and the pen draws its power from that field. The pen therefore needs no battery and no cable. Active ES (usually called AES) uses electrostatic capacitive coupling. The same sensor can also handle finger touch, but the pen must supply its own power.

Three practical differences follow: hover height, whether you think about charging, and whether you can borrow other pens. Because an EMR pen is sensed while still some distance above the surface, hover registers relatively early. An AES pen must get closer. On compatibility, Wacom's official pen list names the LAMY safari twin, STAEDTLER Noris digital, and Kaweco AL SPORT Connect as EMR Digital Writing, meaning they depend on electromagnetic resonance and will not work on an AES display.

The good news is that pens from these four vendors are currently EMR or explicitly labelled battery-free, so "do I have to charge it" stopped being a real question in 2026. Nib wear, grip wear, and whether replacement nibs stay in stock deserve more attention.

What Linux users should know

This section is for Linux readers, because the most widely repeated version of this story is wrong.

The linuxwacom project's own page states that the drivers are pre-installed in most major distributions and provide out-of-the-box support for virtually all Wacom tablets and tablet PCs. The page also documents four packages, each covering a different layer: the input-wacom kernel driver initialises the tablet and translates hardware-specific protocols into standard input events; under Wayland, kernel events are processed by libinput and forwarded to applications, and the project explicitly notes that libinput is external to linuxwacom; under Xorg, xf86-input-wacom virtualises the tablet into logical devices such as stylus, eraser, and touch, reporting extended data including pressure and high-resolution coordinates; and libwacom lets control panels recognise which tablet is currently connected.

The project leaves one caveat: in some cases a model may be too new or too obscure to be fully supported out of the box. Put differently, what you are installing in that situation is not a driver but a backport for hardware your kernel does not yet know about.

The practical test is simple. Plug the tablet in and see whether it is recognised: xsetwacom --list devices under Xorg, or libinput list-devices under Wayland. If your device shows up, the kernel side is already fine. Only reach for configuration when multi-monitor mapping is wrong, in which case xsetwacom set binds the pad to a specific output. That matters most on dual-monitor desks, because the active area otherwise spans every screen and coordinates go haywire.

Of the three, XP-Pen takes Linux most seriously: its site lists .deb, .rpm, and .sh packages for Arch, CentOS, Debian, Fedora, Mageia, Manjaro, Ubuntu and others. Huion restricts support to Ubuntu. Wacom ships no packages, but usually does not need you to.

Troubleshooting

Symptom one: the tablet is plugged in and the system does nothing at all.

Confirm it is not a driver issue before touching drivers. On Windows or macOS, check whether the device manager lists a new HID device. On Linux, run xsetwacom --list devices under Xorg or libinput list-devices under Wayland. No output from either command means the problem is at the system level. A cursor that moves but has no pressure response is a different case, and that is where drivers come in.

Symptom two: the cursor follows the pen but pressure does nothing.

On Windows, close all drawing applications and reinstall the vendor driver. On macOS, check the Accessibility or Privacy list for your drawing application, because since macOS 10.14 unauthorised applications receive no pressure data. This symptom is especially common right after changing drawing software versions. On Linux, get the exact device name from xsetwacom --list devices first, then check whether xf86-input-wacom is missing on the Xorg side.

Symptom three: the pen hovers and the cursor flickers intermittently.

This is usually hover distance, not a fault. The official reading height is 10 mm, and holding the pen at a steep angle or wearing the nib down shortens the effective sensing zone. Fit a standard nib and hold the pen more upright. On pen displays, also check that your hand is not resting on the bezel.

Symptom four: on a multi-monitor desk the active area spans both screens and coordinates are wrong.

This is configuration. Under Xorg, run xsetwacom set "device name" MapToOutput output-name, taking the device name from the earlier listing. Wayland has no xsetwacom, so choose the display output in your desktop's tablet settings. If the aspect ratio still looks wrong after that, it is the mismatch between the pad's aspect ratio and your monitor, which is expected rather than a fault.

Symptom five: the driver installs and nothing changes.

XP-Pen's own installation notes require that you uninstall other brands' drivers and reboot before installing, or you will get conflicts. This matters most when switching from a different brand's tablet, where residue from the previous driver is common.

Frequently asked questions

Q: Can a pen tablet replace the mouse for day-to-day development work?

It can, depending on whether the software honours pressure. In Figma or Draw.io the pen acts as a mouse and pressure does nothing; Krita, Photoshop, and OneNote use it properly. If your workflow is mostly diagrams, the pen changes little.

Q: How much difference is there between 8192 and 16384 pressure levels?

The official figures are simply two numbers, and 8192 is already enough to express almost any line weight in practice. What actually affects the feel is nib material, pen weight distribution, and screen calibration. The return on chasing the number is much smaller than the return on fitting the right nib.

Q: How long does a pen tablet last?

There is no official unified answer, and vendors do not publish MTBF figures. Lifespan depends mostly on nib wear. Pen displays with etched coatings usually wear nibs faster than the textured surface of a screenless pad. Keep a spare nib, because a worn nib visibly degrades pressure response.

Q: Do I need one with a screen?

Ask yourself whether you catch yourself looking down while you draw. If you do, and your neck objects, a pen display solves it. If the work is mostly annotation and framing, a screenless pad at a raised angle is enough.

Q: Are second-hand pen tablets worth buying?

Because the pens are battery-free, pen wear matters more than the board itself. Look for lifted edges and looseness in the pen storage area. Pen displays additionally need a dead-pixel check, which can only be done after delivery. Wacom boards hold value slightly better, partly because driver support is long-lived: Wacom's own guidance is that support for the 2017 generation is expected to end sometime before 2030.
