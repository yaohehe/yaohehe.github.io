Why Programmers Need a USB DAC (Not Just a USB-C to 3.5mm Adapter)

Last month I reviewed 4 USB-C-to-3.5mm analog adapters (Google / Anker / Belkin / UGREEN) at the $8-15 price point. They solve the "MacBook has no headphone jack" problem, but the audio path is:

MacBook USB-C → Analog adapter's tiny built-in DAC (24bit/48kHz, ~90dB SNR) → 3.5mm output

The problem is the adapter's built-in DAC is the bottleneck: 90dB SNR means you'll hear a faint hiss on Sennheiser HD 600 (300Ω) or Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro (80Ω) when nothing is playing.

A dedicated USB DAC solves this with a professional-grade chip:

MacBook USB-C → USB DAC's ES9068AS (32bit/768kHz, DSD256, 130dB SNR) → 3.5mm or 4.4mm balanced → HiFi headphones

You pay 5-10x more than the analog adapter, but you get a real HiFi signal chain.
