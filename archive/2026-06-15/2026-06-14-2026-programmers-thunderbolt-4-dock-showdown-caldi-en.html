Why You Need a Thunderbolt 4 Dock (Not Just a USB-C Hub)

I wrote a 2026 USB-C Hub comparison in June (65W/100W/140W tiers) covering ordinary USB-C multi-port adapters. But if you have a MacBook Pro M2/M3/M4, ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 12, Dell XPS 15/16, or Framework Laptop 16 with Thunderbolt 4 ports, USB-C hubs hit 3 real problems:

1. Insufficient charging power: Regular USB-C hub PD tops out at 100W, but actual laptop delivery is only 65-85W after cable loss + protocol handshake. A MacBook Pro 16" under load will lose charge.

2. Limited external display count: USB-C Alt Mode typically drives only 1-2 displays at 4K@60. Thunderbolt 4 can MST daisy-chain 2 displays at 6K@60 (verified on CalDigit TS4).

3. Half-speed NVMe storage: USB-C hub USB-A 3.0 ports only do 5Gb/s (~500MB/s). Thunderbolt 4 downstream ports run full 40Gb/s (~3000MB/s+). For programmers frequently transferring Git repos, Docker images, and database dumps, that's a 6x difference.

FAQ

Q: My laptop only has USB-C without Thunderbolt 4, can I use a Thunderbolt 4 dock?

A: It works but is wasteful. CalDigit TS4 and Plugable TBT4-UD5 will fall back to USB 3.2 speed (~1GB/s) on plain USB-C laptops. Better to buy a USB4 dock like UGREEN Revodok Pro.

Q: Can MacBook Pro M2 (non Max/Pro) drive dual 6K via CalDigit TS4?

A: No. MacBook Pro M2 chip bandwidth limits external displays to 1× 6K or 2× 4K. Only M3 Pro/M3 Max/M4 Pro/M4 Max supports dual 6K@60.

Q: How stable is Thunderbolt 4 dock under Linux?

A: Plugable TBT4-UD5 on Ubuntu 22.04 LTS / Fedora 39 verified plug-and-play (bolt daemon included in kernel 5.15+). CalDigit TS4 on Linux occasionally needs boltctl enroll to authorize. UGREEN Revodok Pro on Linux needs no drivers (USB protocol).

Q: Are $200-tier Chinese TB4 docks (Anker 778, Baseus 17-in-1) worth it?

A: Anker 778 is actually $249, above $200. Baseus 17-in-1 is USB-C not TB4, speed reduced. Neither has Plugable TBT4-UD5's dual HDMI direct output advantage.
