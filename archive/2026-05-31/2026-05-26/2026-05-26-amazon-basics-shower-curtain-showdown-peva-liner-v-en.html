First: These Are Actually Two Different Products

The PEVA liner and fabric curtain serve completely different purposes. The PEVA liner (sometimes called a shower curtain liner) hangs on the inside of your tub/shower area—its only job is to stop water from splashing out. The fabric curtain hangs on the outside and handles aesthetics and privacy.

You can absolutely use both: fabric curtain on the outside as the main show, PEVA liner on the inside as the waterproof layer. This is standard practice in hotels.

But if you can only buy one, most renters actually only need the PEVA liner.

Buying Guide

Get the PEVA liner if: You're renting, on a tight budget, have poor bathroom ventilation (PEVA is cheap enough to replace every 1-2 years), already have a fabric curtain and just need the waterproof layer.

Get the fabric curtain if: You're a homeowner, care about bathroom style, want one curtain that handles both looks and basic water resistance, hate it when shower curtains billow into you.

The optimal setup: Buy both. Fabric curtain on the outside as the showpiece, PEVA liner on the inside as the waterproof layer. Fabric handles aesthetics, PEVA handles water—each doing what it's best at. This is how most hotels do it, and it's the most comfortable setup.

FAQ

Q: Does the PEVA liner have a plastic smell?

A: Quality PEVA liners like the Amazon Basics model are virtually odorless—it's a PVC-free formula with much less smell than standard PVC alternatives.建议买回来后晾1-2小时再挂.

Q: Can the fabric curtain handle direct water spray?

A: No—not 100% waterproof. Water-resistant fabric is treated with DWR so water beads off, but it's not a plastic barrier. If your shower has high water pressure or you're in there for a long time, you'll want a PEVA liner behind it.

Q: Are these suitable for rental apartments?

A: PEVA liner is perfect for renters—$10, throw it away when you move, no loss. Fabric curtain works for homeowners; for renters, check with your landlord first.

Bottom Line

These two products have completely different use cases. The PEVA liner is about function at the lowest price; the fabric curtain is about upgrading your bathroom experience. Neither is objectively better—it comes down to your actual situation: How's your bathroom ventilation? Do you take long showers? Is there space to dry properly? Those practical questions matter more than brand loyalty.
