Why a Kitchen Scale Changes Everything

Most baking recipes fail not because of bad technique — they fail because of volume-to-weight conversion errors. A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 120g to 150g depending on how you scoop. Weigh it at 125g every time, and your bread turns out identical batch after batch.

Beyond baking, meal prep and portion control benefit equally. If you're tracking macros, accurate gram measurements are non-negotiable.

The Precision Math

1g precision means you're rounding every measurement to the nearest gram. For flour (120-130g per cup), that's a ~0.8% error. Tolerable. For salt (1.2g per teaspoon), you're rounding to whole grams — that's a ~17% error. That matters for fermentation.

If you're baking with yeast and care about salt percentages in your dough ( baker's percentages), 0.1g precision makes a real difference. Otherwise, 1g is fine for most home cooks.
