The Short Version: Pick by Use Case

Go Glass if: You do meal prep, you move food between fridge and oven regularly, and you prioritize seal quality over weight.

Go Airtight Plastic if: You're on a budget, mainly storing dry goods (rice, flour, nuts, snacks), and don't need oven-safe performance.

Go Tritan if: You pack lunch to take to work every day, you care about weight in your bag, and you want a material that won't shatter if dropped.

FAQ

Q: Can the glass containers go in the microwave?

A: Yes, without the lid. Avoid empty-container microwaving — the glass itself is fine but uneven heating can cause food to splatter.

Q: Can I put hot food in the Airtight plastic containers?

A: Not recommended. Let food cool to room temperature before sealing. Hot contents can deform the twist lids over time.

Q: How long before the Tritan gasket needs attention?

A: The gasket itself isn't user-replaceable — Amazon Basics doesn't sell them separately. In my experience, scrubbing with a toothbrush every 3 months keeps gaskets usable for 6-12 months before visible degradation starts.

Q: Which is hardest to clean?

A: Tritan's snap-lock mechanism has more crevices. Glass and Airtight twist lids are straightforward rinse-outs.
