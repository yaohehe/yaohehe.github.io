Why Programmers Need an External SSD

The math is simple:

  • A mid-sized Git repo clone: 2-5GB
  • A VirtualBox VM with dev environment: 30-60GB
  • A machine learning dataset: 50GB to 500GB+

A USB flash drive (30-100MB/s) makes waiting normal. A mechanical external drive (100-120MB/s) is better but still painful. An external SSD (1050MB/s) makes these operations genuinely fast.

But "faster SSD" doesn't mean "most expensive SSD." Your use case determines which one is actually worth buying.

Quick port check:

  • MacBook Pro (2019+): Has Thunderbolt 3 ✓
  • MacBook Air (M1/M2): Has Thunderbolt 4 ✓ (compatible with X5)

FAQ

Q: Can I run a VM from an external SSD?

A: Yes, it works. USB3.2 Gen 2 gives you ~1050MB/s which is fine for most VMs. The X5's Thunderbolt 3 (2800MB/s) makes a noticeable difference if you're running multiple VMs simultaneously or working with disk-intensive IDEs.

Q: Is 1TB enough for a programmer?

A: For most developers, yes. A single code repo is rarely more than a few gigabytes. 1TB holds dozens of projects comfortably. If you're storing large datasets, video素材, or running multiple VMs simultaneously, consider 2TB.

Q: What's the difference between Samsung T7 and T7 Shield?

A: The Shield has IP55 dust/water resistance and a more rugged build. The regular T7 has a slimmer profile but less drop protection. For desk-bound programmers the standard T7 is fine; for those who travel frequently, the Shield is worth the small price premium.

Q: I have a Windows laptop with only USB-C ports. Can I use the Samsung X5?

A: Only if those USB-C ports support Thunderbolt 3. Most business Windows laptops (Dell XPS, ThinkPads) have USB-C Gen 2 ports but no Thunderbolt. In that case the X5 will not work. Stick with T7 or My Passport.
