Installation Reality Check

All three use C-clamp mounting (fits desks up to 3cm thick). Before you start: clear your desk completely, have a flashlight handy, and ignore the included hex key — it's low quality and will strip your screws. Use a proper L-key hex wrench from a hardware store and save yourself 20 minutes of frustration.

VESA plates attach with four screws — standard 75×75 or 100×100mm spacing, no special tools needed. The tricky part is getting the arm tension right on first install: the adjustment dial is usually on the back of the arm near the top. Turn it clockwise to add resistance (needed for heavier monitors), counterclockwise to reduce it (lighter monitors). Start conservatively — you can always add more tension later.

Timing: single arm took me 20 minutes first attempt. Dual arm took 45 minutes because you have to mount the shared base, level it, and then attach both arms with alignment adjustments. If your desk surface isn't perfectly level, the dual arm will constantly tilt — use a cheap bubble level app on your phone before you start.

Cable management: all three have a cable routing channel underneath each arm. Use it. Without it, cables drag on the arm and create unexpected tension that throws off your carefully dialed-in height settings.

3 Mistakes Most Buyers Make

1. Ignoring desk depth

If your desk is less than 60cm deep, the arm's extension will put your monitor beyond comfortable reach. A monitor riser or a short-arm model is a better bet. The "up to 27" spec refers to screen size, not optimal desk depth.

2. Buying the dual arm for one monitor

I see this constantly — people think they're getting a "better deal" with the dual arm at CNY 145 vs CNY 217 for the single. They're not comparable. The dual arm's shared base is specifically designed for two monitors. Using it with one means you're paying for complexity you don't need and fighting installation challenges that don't exist with the single.

3. Maxing out the weight limit

The spec says "holds up to 8.8kg" but that doesn't mean it's comfortable at that limit. At max weight, the gas spring works harder and you'll get more sag over 6+ months. Keep your monitor at 70-80% of the rated weight for long-term stability. If your 27" monitor weighs 8kg+, the reinforced arm (B0CQXL5S4T) is the safer pick even though it's "only" rated to 9.9kg vs 8.8kg.

What the Specs Actually Mean in Practice

"Holds up to 27"" sounds straightforward but it refers to screen diagonal, not the actual monitor depth or weight. A 27" OLED panel weighs 5-7kg; a 27" professional monitor with integrated USB hub can hit 8.5kg+.

If your monitor has an integrated webcam, microphone array, or USB-C dock built in, it's heavier than a standard monitor of the same size. Check the weight spec on your monitor's original box before buying any arm.

The gas spring in B0CQXMT3QC and B0CQXPGNCH uses nitrogen-filled cylinder technology. Over 12-18 months of daily use, the gas pressure naturally decreases — this is normal wear, not defect. You'll notice it as increased "give" when you adjust height. If you start experiencing sag at 18+ months, contact Amazon for a replacement; the warranty covers gas spring degradation.

The mechanical spring in B0CQXL5S4T doesn't have this aging issue — but it also can't be refilled or serviced. When it wears out, you replace the whole arm.
