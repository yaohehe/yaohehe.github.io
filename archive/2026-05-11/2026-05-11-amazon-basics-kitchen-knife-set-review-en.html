Sharpness Test: How's the Edge After 3 Weeks

This is where I spent the most time testing, and it's the metric that matters most for a knife set.

Test 1: Tomato (soft veggie)

  • Day 1: Blade dropped through cleanly, no crushing, juice ran straight down the blade
  • Week 2: Still cuts tomatoes fine, but I noticed I was applying slightly more downward pressure
  • Week 3: Cuts through, but the resistance is noticeably higher. Still usable, just not as satisfying

Test 2: Carrot (firm veggie)

  • Day 1: Clean cut, smooth cross-section, no cracking
  • Week 2: Some minor cracking at the cut edge, blade still functional
  • Week 3: Clear crushing visible. The blade is pushing through carrot rather than slicing it

What this tells us: At week 3 without any honing or sharpening, sharpness dropped approximately 20-25%. For someone who doesn't regularly maintain their knives—which is most home cooks—this is completely normal behavior. I didn't use a honing steel once during the test.

Compare that to a Wüsthof Classic I compared against simultaneously for 6 weeks: barely any perceptible change in cutting performance. That is what better steel and tighter manufacturing tolerances get you.

Build Quality and Maintenance

Amazon Basics construction details:

  • Blade: Stainless steel (specific alloy not disclosed by Amazon)
  • Handle: PP plastic with textured grip pattern
  • Blade style: Western-style chef knife, good for most cutting tasks
  • Weight: Lighter than German knives, which some people prefer

My maintenance routine (for reference):

  • Rinse after each use with warm water
  • Dry immediately with a towel (I skip this sometimes—don't do that)
  • No honing, no oiling, no stropping
  • Stored in the wooden block between uses

The reality: This is a "use and wash" knife set. If you want edge retention beyond 4-6 weeks with daily use, you'll need a $10-15 honing steel. If you want it to hold a razor edge for years, a proper sharpening stone once or twice a year is required.

Good knives need love. Cheap knives need more love. This is not a set you can just put away wet and expect to perform 6 months later.

Who Should Buy This

Buy it if:

  • Budget is strictly under $50 and you need kitchen basics now
  • You are a beginner and do not know what knife styles you prefer yet
  • You are renting and do not want to invest $80+ in tools you might not keep
  • You cook casually—just regular home cooking, not precision work
  • You want a backup set for a second location (garage, vacation home)

Do not buy it if:

  • You do precision work like sashimi preparation or paper-thin slicing
  • You want one set to last 10+ years without babying it
  • You've used quality German knives before and can feel the difference immediately
  • You want that "blade sings through food" feeling when you cut
  • You cook for pleasure and want your tools to feel like an extension of your hands
