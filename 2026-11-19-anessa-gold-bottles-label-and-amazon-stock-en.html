The three lines are separated by technology names, not by SPF numbers

All three carry the same SPF50+ and PA++++, and the official site presents them side by side. What actually separates them is three technology names:

  • Moisture gel NB (パーフェクトUV スキンケアジェル NB): built around Auto Moist Balance, which the official copy describes as holding the moisture balance on the skin surface constant, so an air-conditioned room and a humid street use the same logic. The UV film relies on Aqua Booster EX, which strengthens on contact with sweat and water. It is a gel, and Shiseido states it contains no UV scattering agents, so it is less likely to leave white marks on dark clothing and washes off with soap.
  • Gold-bottle milk NA (パーフェクトUV スキンケアミルク NA): two technologies stacked. Auto Repair repairs the film — the stated reasoning is that smiling, talking and blinking crease the sunscreen film and open gaps, which the technology closes automatically. Auto Booster reacts to sweat, water, heat and moisture in the air. Shake before use.
  • Brightening gel NA (ブライトニングUV ジェル NA): a milky gel with three types of 忍ばせパール and a lavender-pink tint. Shiseido explicitly footnotes that the tone-up effect is makeup effect, not a change in skin colour. The skincare side is the quasi-drug actives: m-tranexamic acid as the brightening active, glycyrrhizinate as the anti-roughness active.

Read together, the division is clean: the milk owns "will the film break," the moisture gel owns "will it feel tacky," and the brightening gel owns "complexion, plus the word 美白."

医薬部外品 buys a sentence, not extra strength

Start with the observation. On the same official site, in the same label slot, the brightening gel reads （医薬部外品）日焼け止め用ジェル while the moisture gel reads 〈日焼け止め用ジェル〉 — the bracket style changes and the five characters in front are gone. Amazon mirrors it: the brightening listing (the older generation) puts 医薬部外品 in its own title, while the moisture gel NB title does not, and offers 乾燥小ジワ instead.

Without the labelling rules, that gap is easy to misread as "one is legitimate, one is not." It actually tracks two filing routes:

  • Ordinary cosmetics are notified, and the claims they may print are limited to the range listed in the health ministry notice.
  • Quasi-drugs are approved product by product, contain actives designated by the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare, and can therefore print sentences cosmetics cannot.

Item 25 of MHLW Notification No. 25 designates as quasi-drugs those "used for the purposes in Article 2, Paragraph 3 plus the prevention of acne, rough skin, rash, chilblains and similar, or disinfection of skin or oral cavity." The brightening gel printing 肌あれ防止有効成分 lands on exactly that item.

So the order of reading should be: does the box say 医薬部外品, then what extra sentence does that let it print, then is that sentence useful to you. Done the other way round — spotting 有効成分 and buying — you end up paying more for a one-star water resistance because of the word 美白.

Of the 56 sentences a cosmetic may print, number 37 already covers spots

This is the most interesting layer of the whole check. The MHLW notice 化粧品の効能の範囲の改正について (薬食発 0721 No. 1, 21 July 2011) attaches a table of 56 permitted claims. Read straight through, two things stand out.

First, sun protection and spot prevention are already in the table: item 36 日やけを防ぐ and item 37 日やけによるシミ、ソバカスを防ぐ. An ordinary cosmetic can legally print "prevents sun-caused spots and freckles" without becoming a quasi-drug. The 未来のシミまで防ぐ line on the moisture gel NB sits on item 37.

Second, nowhere in the table does it say "suppresses melanin production." The closest is item 37's qualifier 日やけによる — limited to sunlight as the cause. The brightening gel's メラニンの生成を抑え、シミ・そばかすを防ぐ extends the mechanism to melanin itself, which is not in the 56, and that is precisely why it needs the quasi-drug route.

Put the two together and 医薬部外品 gets a more accurate definition: not a stamp for stronger effect, but a licence to speak at the melanin level. The same notice added item 56, 乾燥による小ジワを目立たなくする, in that 2011 revision — and the 乾燥小ジワ in the Amazon title of the moisture gel NB is using that item. That is where the anti-ageing vocabulary of an ordinary cosmetic stops.

One commonly repeated error worth correcting: sunscreen in Japan does not equal quasi-drug. Two of these three bottles are ordinary cosmetics; only the brightening one is not. I went through the same classification logic for Santen FX eye drops and the 2nd-class versus 3rd-class split; the principle carries over — the class sets what you may claim, not how strong the formula is.

The 40 g travel size is a 34 percent premium, not a saving

The same arithmetic points the other way on the small bottle. 40 g at ¥1,496 is 37.4 yen per gram, 34 percent above the 27.9 of the 90 g.

The premium is not a scam — filling and packaging a small tube genuinely costs more, and a 40 g tube in a bag makes sense. Where each bottle actually breaks

No pros-and-cons list here, only boundaries the official pages admit, because those decide whether you buy the wrong bottle:

  • The milk breaks on capacity and price. One size, 60 mL, the highest unit price of the three. Its 最強 footnote is explicit: it means SPF50+, PA++++ and Anessa's internal UV water-resistance rating — a within-brand definition, not a cross-brand conclusion.

If you are getting wet, this is not the first pick — and it happens to be the bottle with the loudest claims.

Three things to check before you buy

  • Whether the box says 医薬部外品. If yes, it bought the right to claim melanin suppression. If no, it can still prevent sunburn and sun-caused spots — items 36 and 37 already cover that.
  • The unit price. Divide the price by the stated volume. Grams against millilitres here is only an order-of-magnitude comparison, but 27.9 against 51.0 is a gap wide enough that finer correction is unnecessary.
  • Which generation Amazon is actually selling. The official site pushes NB and NA; the brightening line on Amazon is still N with a 旧モデル tag attached. If generation matters, wait for the new one or buy direct; if it does not, the old generation is usually priced looser.

FAQ

Q: Is a 医薬部外品 more effective than an ordinary cosmetic?

That is not the axis. What it adds is claim scope — it can print "suppresses melanin production, prevents spots and freckles." Ordinary cosmetics may already print item 37, preventing sun-caused spots. Among these three, the quasi-drug one has the lowest UV water resistance.

Q: Can the three bottles be layered?

Shiseido gives no layering protocol, and all three are face-and-body sunscreens with overlapping roles. The leaner approach is one bottle per day: milk for water, moisture gel for indoors, brightening gel when complexion is the concern.

Q: Why is the new brightening gel missing on Amazon?

None of the four keyword sets surfaced an NA standalone page; the top slot was the 旧モデル N. Possible causes are a distribution lag or a kit-only channel, and I found no official statement, so the honest wording is "not found in search," not "not sold." Buying direct from the official store is the safer route if you need the new generation.

Q: Is the 40 g travel size worth it?

Depends on why. To keep one in a bag, yes. To save money, no — 37.4 yen per gram against 27.9 is 34 percent more, and two 40 g tubes cost more than one 90 g.

Q: Why no single price figure?

This machine exits from China, and Amazon.co.jp renders prices in US dollars on that route (the moisture gel displayed USD 13.86 and the milk USD 15.73 at fetch time, for example), not in yen. The yen figures here are Shiseido's reference retail prices; the amount Amazon charges is whatever your own page shows.

Closing

Choosing among the three gold bottles is a scenario question, not a spec question. On a day with sweat and water, the milk's self-repairing film earns its price. For fifteen minutes of commuting, the 90 g moisture gel is the cheapest per gram and the easiest to wash off. If the goal is a less sallow complexion, that is when you reach for the bottle marked 医薬部外品 — knowing that what you paid extra for is the right to say 美白, not more sun protection.

If it has to be one sentence: decide how long you will be outside, then pick the bottle, then look at the tag. Done backwards, you end up carrying a one-star brightening gel to the beach.
