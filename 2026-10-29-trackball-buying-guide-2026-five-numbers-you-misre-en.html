Trackball Specs 2026: Five Numbers to Misread

trackballergonomicsbuying guide

Trackball spec sheets are among the easiest to misread in desktop gear. The same "512-2048 dpi" string appears twice on Logitech's official pages, once for the MX Ergo and once for the MX Ergo S, yet only the MX Ergo row carries an extra line: "Nominal value: 380 dpi" — a number roughly 26% below the 512 minimum that same table claims. Battery life does something similar: the MX Ergo says "up to 4 months", the MX Ergo S says "up to 120 days". It reads like an upgrade. It is the same number with a different unit.

This article does not answer "thumb ball or finger ball". The trackball guide published in July covers that trade-off, and the vertical mouse piece already covers static hand posture and wrist angle. This one works at a different layer: it looks only at numbers the manufacturers publish themselves, and at the conditions they print somewhere else. The method matches the digital caliper guide and the clamp meter guide: do not compare magnitudes, compare what each figure is measured under.

Boundaries first. Every number below comes from a manufacturer product page, an official support page, or an official press release, captured on 2026-10-29. Nothing here was measured on hardware. TL;DR: settle three things before you look at models. First, which hand? Both the MX Ergo and MX Ergo S official FAQs state they are "only manufactured in a right-handed configuration", so left-handed buyers should start with Kensington or ELECOM. Second, do you want the tilt? The 20-degree angle on the MX Ergo line comes from a magnetic metal plate, and fitting it takes the device from 164 g to 259 g — so do not judge portability from the 164 g figure. Third, accept that official pages contradict themselves: the MX Ergo S marketing copy says "20-degree tilt angle" while its own FAQ says the hinge is adjustable between 0 and 20 degrees. Number one: 380 is below 512 — the nominal value is outside the stated range

The MX Ergo sensor row gives three values: Logitech Advanced Optical Tracking, a nominal value of 380 dpi, and a min/max of 512-2048 dpi. The problem sits between the last two. By definition, min/max is the adjustable range, so the nominal value should fall inside it. 380 does not.

That is more than a layout quirk. dpi means pixels moved per inch (25.4 mm) of ball surface travel, so dividing the nominal value by the stated minimum gives the gap between the factory default step and the lowest adjustable step: 380 / 512 is about 0.742. In other words, at the default setting the same finger travel moves the cursor about 74% as far as it would at the lowest setting — roughly 26% less. If you sized up fine-control feel from "minimum 512", what ships is 380.

The MX Ergo S drops the row entirely and keeps only "min/max 512-2048 dpi". Logitech does not say why, and I am not going to guess. What is certain: the adjustable range is identical across both generations, and only the older model publishes a nominal value. For buying purposes that is simpler — with the refresh there is no factory default to guess at.

The entry-level siblings are worth noting too. Logitech's China store lists ERGO M575 as "rated 400 dpi, maximum up to 2000", while the official support page lists ERGO M575S as "DPI (min/max): 100 - 2,000". Same series, same generation, three different notations. Check which one you are reading before comparing across models.

Number two: "4 months" and "120 days" are the same figure, and the M575S lasts less than the M575

At 30 days per month, 4 months is 120 days, which matches the MX Ergo S figure exactly. Battery endurance did not change between the two generations; only the unit did.

The entry tier did change. Logitech's China store lists ERGO M575 at "up to 24 months with the USB receiver, up to 20 months over Bluetooth". The official support page lists ERGO M575S at "up to 18 months" from a single AA cell. Same one AA cell, same thumb-ball line, and the official endurance figure dropped from 24 months to 18 months with the refresh. That page also offers a possible factor: it publishes wireless report rates of "125 Hz (8 ms) in LO mode" and "88-133 Hz over Bluetooth", figures the M575 page does not give. A higher report rate usually costs power, but Logitech does not state that causal link, so I am placing the two official numbers side by side rather than drawing the inference.

One more caveat: both endurance claims carry a footnote. The MX Ergo says "Battery life may vary based on user and computing conditions"; the MX Ergo S says "It may vary based on use and computing conditions". A footnoted battery figure is a ceiling, not an expectation.

Number three: between 164 g and 259 g sits one metal plate

Both MX Ergo pages list two weights: 164 g without the metal plate and without the receiver, 259 g with the metal plate and without the receiver. That is 95 g, about a 58% increase.

The plate is not an accessory; it is how the tilt works. It attaches magnetically and lifts the whole unit to 20 degrees. The MX Ergo S FAQ describes it as "a unique adjustable hinge lets you tilt the trackball angle between 0 and 20 degrees", so both the 0-degree and 20-degree positions depend on this piece. If you want the tilt, you carry the 95 g.

That splits buyers cleanly. Anyone who commutes with the device in a bag is carrying 259 g, roughly a phone more than the headline figure. Anyone whose device never leaves the desk gets the stability as a bonus. Decide whether it travels before you decide whether it is heavy.

Number four: Unifying and Bolt do not interoperate, and official pages get this wrong

The MX Ergo connects over a Unifying USB receiver plus Bluetooth Low Energy 4.1. The MX Ergo S uses a Logi Bolt USB receiver plus Bluetooth Low Energy, with no version published. Logitech's own support article on the differences lists it explicitly: "MX Ergo S uses the Logi BOLT receiver, while MX Ergo uses the Logi Unifying receiver."

The practical effect is that an existing Unifying receiver will not adopt the new device, and vice versa. If your desk already runs a Unifying setup — an MX Keys, an older MX Master — moving to the MX Ergo S means either giving up another USB port or living with two receivers.

The more useful lesson is that Logitech's own page contradicts itself on this field. In the ERGO M575S specification, "What's in the box" lists a "Logi Bolt receiver", while the "Battery life" line on the same page reads "up to 18 months using the included Unifying USB receiver". Both protocols appear in one official document. Pick either column and leave room for doubt. The handling is the same one used in the ergonomic chair decision tree: when official sources disagree, publish both quotes and plan for the more conservative one.

Number five: 20% and 27% cannot be subtracted, because they measure different things

The MX Ergo copy reads "creates 20% less muscle fatigue in your hand, wrist, and forearm than a regular mouse" — the comparison is against an ordinary mouse, the sites measured are hand, wrist and forearm, and the metric is fatigue.

The MX Ergo S copy reads "reduces muscle strain in the forearm by 27%" — no comparator is stated, the site is only the forearm, and the metric changed from fatigue to strain.

Three variables moved at once, so 27 minus 20 does not yield 7, and nothing here supports "the S is 7% better". Each figure has to be quoted on its own, with its own qualifiers attached.

By contrast, the quiet-click claim is fully specified. The support article says "80% quieter than the MX Ergo", and the product page footnote adds that this is a comparison between the two models, "measured by Logitech's internal lab at 1 metre, dBA, on left click". On the same page, the claim with a footnote is safe to quote; the one without needs its qualifiers supplied by you.

FAQ

Q: Is the tilt on the MX Ergo S adjustable like the MX Ergo?

Yes. The official FAQ says "a unique adjustable hinge lets you tilt the trackball angle between 0 and 20 degrees". Note that the marketing copy only mentions a "20-degree tilt angle"; the 0-degree position appears solely in the FAQ. The two wordings differ, the conclusion does not.

Q: I am left-handed. Can I use the MX Ergo S?

No. The FAQ states "the MX Ergo S is currently only manufactured in a right-handed configuration". Ambidextrous options are the Kensington Orbit with Scroll Ring, listed as Ambidextrous, and the ELECOM HUGE.

Q: Does 20% versus 27% mean the S is kinder to my wrist?

Not on this evidence. The measurement sites differ (hand, wrist and forearm versus forearm only), the metrics differ (fatigue versus strain), and the MX Ergo S figure comes with no published method. The only fully specified claim on that page is the noise one: internal lab, 1 metre, dBA, left click.

Q: I have a keyboard on a Unifying receiver. Do I need to re-pair if I switch to the MX Ergo S?

You need a second receiver. Logitech's support page states the MX Ergo S uses Logi Bolt and the MX Ergo uses Logi Unifying; the two protocols do not adopt each other's devices, so the Bolt receiver takes an additional USB port.

Q: Why can I find a model on the official site but not on Amazon?

That is normal, not an exception. Kensington's own page for the SlimBlade Pro (K72080WW) reads "This product is no longer available", and both Amazon listings for it (B0BLRQ4116, B0DK8XVVST) showed Currently unavailable when captured. The Kensington Expert Mouse Wireless (K72359WW) appears on Amazon only bundled with another model. A specification table existing does not mean a standalone listing exists today.

Q: How do you clean a trackball?

Follow the manufacturer. The MX Ergo S FAQ says to "Remove the magnetic metal base plate and use a non-metallic pen or rod to push the trackball out from the bottom hole to wipe down the socket and sensor". ELECOM says the HUGE ball can be pushed out through the hole in the base.
