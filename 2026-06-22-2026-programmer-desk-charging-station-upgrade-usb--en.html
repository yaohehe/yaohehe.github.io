Why Programmers Need USB-C PD Surge Protectors

Regular power strip USB-A ports deliver 5W/2.4A max — barely enough for a phone, completely insufficient for a laptop. USB-C PD (Power Delivery) pushes 65W or even 100W through a single cable, letting you ditch your laptop's DC brick entirely.

Surge protection matters especially for programmers: development machines, monitors, external SSDs, and NAS devices can cost thousands combined. One surge event without protection means replacing all of them.

FAQ

Q: Is 30W USB-C PD enough for a gaming laptop or workstation?

A: No. 30W only works for 13-14" thin-and-light laptops (MacBook Air, ThinkPad X1, Dell XPS 13). Gaming laptops and workstations typically need 65W or 96W+ adapters. For those, keep your original charger and use the power strip for AC outlets only.

Q: What does the joule rating actually mean?

A: Higher joules = more surge energy absorption capacity. 1,000J+ protects standard electronics; 1,500J+ is recommended for high-value equipment (dev workstation, monitor, NAS). All three options here exceed 1,000J — this is the minimum safe baseline for a programmer's desk.

Q: Is wall-mount installation complicated?

A: The Amazon Basics unit includes mounting screws and wall anchors. Installing it behind or under your desk takes about 5 minutes. Some users opt for 3M Command strips for damage-free, removable installation.
