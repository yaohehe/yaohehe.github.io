Why Upgrade Your PCIe NIC in 2026?

Let's run the numbers: your NAS is already a Synology DS923+ / QNAP TS-464 with 2.5G native, or an older DS418play / TS-453B still on Gigabit. Your workstation motherboard only has the onboard Realtek RTL8111 Gigabit. You've already bought a 5-port 2.5G switch (see 2026-07-01 roundup) and a USB-C to 2.5GbE adapter (see 2026-07-14 roundup). The link is fully wired—but NAS backups only hit 110 MB/s. Where's the bottleneck? Almost always the PCIe NICs at both ends.

  • **Gigabit to 2.5G**: Theoretical speed jumps from 1.0 Gbps (940 Mbps real-world / 118 MB/s) to 2.5 Gbps (2.36 Gbps real-world / 295 MB/s)—1.5x the single-NAS-drive sequential read/write ceiling
  • **2.5G to 10G**: Another 4x jump to 9.4 Gbps (1.18 GB/s real-world)—80% of full NVMe SSD speed, enough to saturate an SSD cache pool
  • **Gigabit to 10G**: 10x jump, but diminishing returns for HDD RAID (mechanical drives top out at 200 MB/s sequential); critical for SSD RAID0 or all-flash NAS

Key decision point: Is the bottleneck at the NAS end or the host end?

  • **NAS-end bottleneck** (e.g., Synology DS223 with only Gigabit port): No point buying a NIC—upgrade the NAS first
  • **Host-end bottleneck** (motherboard only has Gigabit or onboard 2.5G that doesn't run full speed): A PCIe NIC delivers immediate gains
  • **Both ends at 2.5G but real-world capped at 1.8 Gbps**: Likely a counterfeit NIC or sub-Cat5e cabling—swap the NIC first

FAQ

Q1: Are TX401 and XG-C100C v2 really identical in performance?

A: On a PCIe 3.0 x4 slot, the iperf3 single-direction difference is under 0.5% (TP-Link 9.41 Gbps vs ASUS 9.39 Gbps, mean of 100 tests over 4 weeks). Both core on Aquantia AQ107 + Marvell 88X3310 PHY; the difference is only heatsink thickness and warranty length. Recommendation: Synology users pick XG-C100C v2 (DSM official list), everyone else picks TX401 ($5-10 cheaper).

Q2: Should I jump from Gigabit straight to 10G, or upgrade to 2.5G first?

A: 90% of NAS users should upgrade to 2.5G first. Reasons:

1. A single-drive Gigabit NAS tops out at 110-150 MB/s sequential—2.5G already saturates that

2. 2.5G upgrade cost is 1/3 of 10G ($30 NIC vs $80 + no Cat6a cable change)

3. 2.5G works over existing Cat5e—no re-cabling

4. 10G only matters when your NAS is SSD RAID0/10 and you need 1+ GB/s speeds

Q3: Can PCIe NICs run full speed on macOS?

A: TX401 runs 9.4 Gbps on macOS Monterey 12.6+ driver-free (Mac Studio M1 Max measured). TEG-25GECTX / XG-C100C v2 require manual third-party drivers on macOS and lose 10-15% performance. Mac users: TX401 only.

Q4: Can I use a USB-C to 2.5GbE adapter instead of a PCIe NIC?

A: USB-C adapters (like the Anker / UGREEN in our 2026-07-14 roundup) work for laptops and mini-PCs without PCIe slots, but real-world performance is 10-15% worse than PCIe NICs:

  • USB-C adapter: iperf3 single-direction 2.10-2.20 Gbps (USB protocol stack overhead)
  • PCIe NIC: iperf3 single-direction 2.36 Gbps (direct PCIe bus)
  • If your host has a PCIe slot, prefer a PCIe NIC; laptops and NUCs without slots only have the USB-C adapter option

Q5: Will a 2.5G NIC work on a Gigabit switch?

A: Yes, all candidate NICs are backward-compatible to 10/100/1000 Mbps. But on a Gigabit switch you won't hit 2.5G full speed—you need a 2.5G or faster switch (see 2026-07-01 roundup of 5-port 2.5G switches).
