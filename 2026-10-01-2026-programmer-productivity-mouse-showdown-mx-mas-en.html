Why a programmer's mouse deserves its own shortlist

A keyboard can be re-sprung, re-capped and re-flashed. A mouse is buy-and-live-with-it. Programmers also stress a mouse differently from general office users: long left-button drags to select text, constant horizontal scrolling through wide code lines, and dozens of context switches a day between editor, terminal, browser and debugger. Those three behaviours map onto exactly three design variables — button layout, horizontal scrolling, and the software layer that handles cross-device and per-app switching.

So this is not a ranking. It is a breakdown of four distinct routes: the new flagship, the proven previous generation, the compact travel mouse, and the vertical ergonomic intervention. They solve different problems, and picking the wrong route hurts far more than picking the wrong brand.

Troubleshooting: four errors I actually ran into

What stops people after purchase is rarely the spec sheet — it is the software layer. These four reproduced reliably in my testing.

Error 1: Logi Options+ sees the mouse, but custom buttons do nothing.

Cause: on macOS, Options+ needs Input Monitoring permission to intercept button events; on Windows, it loses the ability to hook into some fullscreen apps unless it runs with administrator rights. Fix: on macOS open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, find Input Monitoring, tick Logi Options+ and restart the app. On Windows, tick "Run as administrator" in the shortcut properties, or set the mouse driver service to start automatically.

Error 2: The MX Master 4 receiver will not fit the case, or does nothing when plugged in.

Cause: this generation's Logi Bolt receiver is USB-C, and Logitech requires an available USB-C port. If you plug in an older Unifying receiver it will never be recognised — the two protocols are not interchangeable. Fix: use a USB-C to USB-A adapter on older desktops, or switch to Bluetooth. You can also plug the Logi Bolt receiver into a monitor's USB-C hub port, but the monitor must support data passthrough.

Error 3: The cursor drifts on a glass desk, or stops working entirely after you add a screen protector film.

Cause: Darkfield tracks glass, but only within limits of thickness and surface pattern. Thick glass, frosted glass or patterned glass defeats it. Fix: a cloth mouse pad is the simplest answer. If you insist on the bare surface, clean it first — oil films and dust break tracking just as effectively.

Error 4: After switching between three devices, the cursor stutters for a second or two.

Cause: a 2.4 GHz receiver interferes with USB 3.0 ports, Wi-Fi cards and Bluetooth, especially when the receiver sits in a front-panel port or next to an external drive. Fix: move the Logi Bolt receiver to a rear port, use an extension cable to put distance between it and USB 3.0 devices and your router, or standardise on Bluetooth to remove one 2.4 GHz channel from the equation.

FAQ

Q: Is the price gap between the MX Master 4 and MX Master 3S worth it?

A: Only if you will use the Actions Ring and haptics. If you mostly alternate between an editor and a terminal, the 3S wheel and sensor are already more than enough. If you hop between five or six apps dozens of times a day, the ring overlay saves real movement. As of writing the gap is roughly $30.

Q: Does the MX Anywhere 3S work on a glass desk?

A: Yes — Logitech specifies tracking on glass and other smooth surfaces, and the 8K DPI sensor holds precision at low sensitivity. Very thick glass or textured glass will still defeat it, so test on your own desk first.

Q: How long does it take to adapt to the MX Vertical?

A: I needed about eight days to get back to full speed. Logitech and most reviewers say one to two weeks. Switch during a low-pressure stretch, not before a release.

Q: Do these mice work properly on Linux?

A: Basic functions do. Movement, clicks, wheel and back/forward are usually driver-free on mainstream distributions. But Logi Options+ ships for Windows and macOS only, so per-app button mapping, cross-computer copy-paste and the Actions Ring are effectively unavailable on Linux.

Q: If I only buy one, which should it be?

A: If your wrist is fine and your budget is under a hundred dollars, the MX Master 3S is the lowest-risk choice. If the budget is tighter still, drop to Amazon Basics and accept the lack of a horizontal wheel and the coarse scroll detents.
