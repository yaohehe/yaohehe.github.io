One: lay the three spec sheets side by side and the problem appears immediately

What I actually did was pull each vendor's official spec page and copy the figures into a table one by one, rather than repeating conclusions from second-hand reviews. Once the table was full, one thing was obvious: these three vendors do not share a ruler.

Butterfly is the most economical: a plain integer, no unit, no test method. DHS's official catalogue prints "Hardness 38/39/40/41" with no unit either. Tibhar's official page prints "HARDNESS 45,7 – 47,7°" with a decimal comma, a degree symbol and, crucially, a range.

Put the three notations next to each other and you understand why "which one is harder" cannot be answered with a number. It is not that any of them is sloppy. Each built a ruler inside its own factory and none of them lent it out.

This is also why the claim "Butterfly's 36 plays harder than Hurricane's 39" circulates in Chinese forums: those two numbers were never on the same axis to begin with.

Four: Tibhar's 45,7 – 47,7, the decimal comma, and a "50 and up" claim that does not exist

Tibhar's official shop lists MX-P as "HARDNESS 45,7 – 47,7°", and the 2025/2026 official catalogue PDF gives the same range. The sibling EL-P is 42,4 – 44,4°.

Two things are easy to misread here.

First, this is a tolerance band, not two models. The 45,7 to 47,7 range means measurements of one sheet land inside that span. It is not a "45.7 version" and a "47.7 version". Read it as a centre of 46.7 plus or minus one, not as a menu you can pick from.

Second, the widely repeated claim that Tibhar starts at 50 degrees is false. MX-P is 45.7 to 47.7 and EL-P is 42.4 to 44.4, neither above 50. Tibhar does sell an MX-P 50, but that is a separate harder variant, not the floor of the range. The weekly plan I was working from originally said "Tibhar's whole line starts at 50 degrees". I checked the official pages and deleted that line, because it is wrong.

There is a messier layer too. Tibhar prints speed 125, spin 120 and control 80. Butterfly prints 83 and 76. Nittaku prints 10.25 and 15.00. Even the speed figure runs on three different rulers. So when a review tells you "this sheet is 20 points faster than that one", in most cases someone subtracted numbers from two different vendors, and the difference means nothing.

Five: what to do when the numbers collide, 40 against 40

Dignics 05 is officially hardness 40. Hurricane 3's 40-degree grade is also 40. Same number, two entirely different objects.

Dignics 05 is a tension-type, non-tacky sheet built on Spring Sponge X, with the maker emphasising deformation and rebound. Hurricane 3 is a tacky Chinese-style sheet that grips the ball through surface tack, on a harder sponge that rewards your own active power. Lining them up to ask "whose 40 is harder" is like comparing Celsius and Fahrenheit by which number is bigger.

In practice I would give you an ordering that uses no numbers at all. Ask whether your swing can bottom out a hard sponge. Then ask whether you want the held-ball feel that a tacky topsheet gives you. Answer those two and you have already chosen. The remaining numbers only confirm it.

Seven: the cost maths, one sheet inside the duty-free line and two outside it

At the 2 November 2026 rate, one US dollar buys roughly 157.9 yen. Japanese customs states that where the dutiable value is 10,000 yen or less, customs duty and consumption tax are waived. Above that, 60 percent of the dutiable value becomes the tax base, and duty plus 10 percent consumption tax are applied on top.

Take Nittaku Kyouhyou Pro 3. The official price is ¥5,000 before tax, ¥5,500 including tax. One sheet stays inside the ¥10,000 line and remains duty-free. Two sheets come to ¥11,000 and break it. A sheet covers one side of the blade, and a forehand plus backhand setup already means two sheets, so breaking the line is effectively the default. Do not estimate your landed cost from a single-sheet calculation.

Two exceptions and one expiry date are worth memorising. Leather goods and leather-soled footwear are excluded from that exemption. And the Ministry of Finance amendment removes the small-parcel import exemption from 1 April 2028. Rubber is not leather footwear, so it enjoys the line today, but the line itself has an end date.

FAQ

Q: Is Butterfly's 36 really harder than Hurricane's 39?

A: The question cannot be asked that way. They are two rulers: Butterfly uses its own integer scale, Hurricane uses Chinese hardness grades. The Japanese official page gives one anchor, 42.5 corresponding to a China basis of 39.0, which puts Hurricane 39 around 42.5 on the Japanese scale, above Butterfly's 36. But that is a labelling correspondence published by a vendor, not a promise that they feel the same.

Q: Why do so many models in one brand share exactly the same hardness?

A: Because hardness is only one parameter of the sponge, and vendors use it to build tiers. The entire main Tenergy line is 36 and the entire FX line is 32; feel is separated by pimple geometry (No.05, No.64, No.180, No.219) and sponge formulation, not by hardness grades.

Q: Amazon.co.jp does not show yen for me. How do I work out the landed cost?

A: This machine's exit geolocation is outside Japan, so the site displays another currency. Work it out yourself as official yen price times exchange rate, plus international shipping, plus any duty and consumption tax. Do not treat the displayed figure as the transaction price.

Q: Does rubber expire, and can I stockpile it?

A: No vendor publishes a shelf life for rubber, but the internal tension in a tension-type sponge does decay over time. That is physics, not a manufacturer promise. Keep stockpiling to less than one season's worth.

Q: Why are there no purchase links for Dignics 05 and 09C?

A: On Amazon.co.jp I only found bundled set pages for those two, with no standalone page whose product title I could parse. Under this site's rules, a model I cannot verify does not get a recommendation slot.
