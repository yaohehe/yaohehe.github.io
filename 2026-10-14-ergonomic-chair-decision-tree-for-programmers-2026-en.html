Back pain that needs a doctor is a different problem. Sitting eight hours a day in the wrong chair while you write code is a problem most programmers can fix themselves and keep postponing. Why fit matters more than comfort

However many adjustment mechanisms a chair has, all it does is make a handful of geometric values adjustable: seat height, seat depth, lumbar height and depth, armrest height and width, recline angle. Outside those ranges, spending more does not buy you anything.

The first commonly ignored value is minimum seat height. SIHOO's official tech specs put the Doro C300 at 47–55 cm (18.50–21.65 in), so the floor is nearly 47 cm. Someone around 160 cm tall may not get their heels flat on the floor. At that point the lumbar system does not matter, because you slide forward and lose the support anyway. HON's Ignition 2.0, by contrast, is listed at 17"–21.5" on hon.com and matches on Staples and Office Depot listings — about 1.5 in lower at the bottom, which is friendlier to shorter users.

The second is whether back height is adjustable. HON's official spec sheet states it plainly: Back Height Adjustable: No. The Ignition 2.0 is a mid-back model with a fixed back, which means the vertical position of the lumbar pad is nailed down. A taller person cannot raise it to their own lumbar level. You will not find this on a product hero image; it only lives in the spec table.

The third is the weight rating. The same brand routinely publishes different numbers on different regional sites: SIHOO lists the Doro C300 at 136 kg (300 lb) on its EU site and 330 lbs on its US site. That is more than a typo, and there is a whole section on it below.

The decision tree: four branches to your chair

Branch 1: measure your height against seat height, not "recommended height"

Manufacturer height ranges carry marketing weight and often disagree between pages. The harder number is the seat height range.

  • Height ≤ 165 cm → look for a minimum seat height of 46 cm or less, otherwise your feet will not plant. The SIHOO M18 at 44–54 cm is comparatively friendly here
  • Height 165–180 cm → the sweet spot for most mid-range models: SIHOO M57, Doro C300 and HBADA E3 Air all cover it
  • Height ≥ 185 cm → look for models whose official range reaches 195 cm, such as the HBADA E3 Air 2026 Edition (official range 5'1"–6'5")

Branch 2: body weight sets the ceiling, so read the rating before the lumbar

  • Up to 136 kg (300 lb) → every mid-range model here sits inside its official rating
  • Over 136 kg → you need a higher-rated chair. For reference, Herman Miller rates the Aeron at 300 lb for size A, 350 lb for size B and 400 lb for size C on store.hermanmiller.com; the EMEA product sheet's 136 / 159 / 181 kg figures match
  • ⚠️ Ratings differ between regional sites of the same brand, so trust the listing you are actually ordering from

Branch 3: how long you sit decides mesh versus foam

  • Under 4 hours a day → a foam seat is acceptable; the SIHOO M18 is in this group (official spec: Soft sponge cushion)
  • 4–8 hours a day → go full mesh: the SIHOO M57 and Doro C300 both use mesh seats
  • You recline for naps → check maximum recline: Doro C300 is listed at 110°/120°/130°, while the HBADA E3 Air 2026 Edition goes to five positions and a maximum of 140°

Branch 4: land the budget on three tiers

  • Entry: SIHOO M18, SIHOO M57
  • Mid-range: SIHOO Doro C300, HBADA E3 Air 2026 Edition
  • Contract furniture and high end: HON Ignition 2.0, Herman Miller Aeron

Fallback branch: when none of the three resolve

Choose a mid-range model whose brand offers a trial or return window and sit in it for 30 days. The reason: whether the lumbar pad matches your spinal curve is something only a full working day tells you. No spec table substitutes for it. HBADA's official page states a 30-day in-home trial; SIHOO's official FAQ gives 30-day returns. Building those two facts into the decision matters more than spending another two hundred dollars.

FAQ

Q: I am 175 cm and 70 kg, coding eight hours a day. Which one should I look at?

A: Start with seat height. The Doro C300's 47–55 cm range fits you, and although its recommended height appears as both 150–180 and 155–190 cm, 175 cm falls inside both. Its adaptive lumbar suits people who shift posture often; if you would rather pin the lumbar at one fixed spot, the M57's manually adjustable two-way lumbar gives you more control.

Q: Can I trust the manufacturer's "recommended height"?

A: Use it as a hint, not as a criterion. In the checklist above, one SIHOO model carries three different height ranges across three sites. The hard numbers are the seat height range and seat depth range: measure your lower leg from the back of the knee to the floor and compare it with the minimum seat height.

Q: Is adaptive lumbar better than manually adjustable lumbar?

A: It depends on whether you will actually adjust it. Adaptive systems (Doro C300, HBADA E3 Air) are effortless but remove your ability to lock the pad. Manual systems (M57, M18, HON Ignition 2.0) take ten or fifteen minutes to dial in and then stay put. If you will not fiddle, pick adaptive; if you will, pick manual.

Q: Why do the brand site and the marketplace listing show different specs for the same chair?

A: Three common reasons: different generations (HBADA's 3rd generation versus the 2026 Edition is exactly this), different regional sites using different units and test standards (rounding between lb and kg turns 300 into 330), and pages that were never updated after a product change. Take the listing's spec as final and screenshot it.

Q: On a tight budget, will an entry-tier chair fall apart over a long day?

A: The M18's specs lean toward under four hours a day. If you realistically sit more than six hours, move the budget to the M57 or the Doro C300 — the full mesh seat and the 3D or 4D armrests make a concrete difference over long sessions.

Q: What if it does not fit once it arrives?

A: Policies differ by brand and change over time. SIHOO's official FAQ gives 30-day returns and HBADA's page states a 30-day in-home trial. Re-check the current policy on the product page before ordering rather than relying on this article.

Closing

Specs can narrow this category from dozens of chairs down to three or four, but the last step is sitting in one for 30 days. The order I recommend: measure your height and weight, walk the four branches above, pick a mid-range model with a return window, then give yourself a month of observation.

A chair only solves sitting. Desk height and screen height are the other two links in the same chain, so if you are rebuilding the whole desk, the monitor arm and standing desk articles on this site are the natural next read.
