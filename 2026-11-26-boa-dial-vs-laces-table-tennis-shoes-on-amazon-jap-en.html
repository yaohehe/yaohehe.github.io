The dial tightens in steps and does not loosen in steps

BOA keeps a product page for this exact shoe. It states the model carries the L6 dial, and lists three actions: push in to engage, turn to tighten, pull up for fast release.

There is no fourth action. Shimano's official BOA parts guide splits the L-Series into two groups in plain language. L6, L5 and L4 sit under "PRECISION FIT + FAST RELEASE", with two moves: turn to tighten, pull up for fast release. Only L+1, Li2 and IP1 get the "MULTIDIRECTIONAL PRECISION FIT" label, which adds a third move: turn to loosen.

So the precision is one-directional. You can add tension in small increments. You cannot remove tension in small increments. To go looser you pull the dial up, release everything, and wind back to where you want to be.

That difference is concrete on court. Third game, your instep is swelling. With laces you slack the top run one eyelet and you are done in two seconds. With L6 you either wait for the interval or dump the whole dial and rebuild the tension. Between points, the second option is not realistic.

"BOA lets you fine-tune any time" is half true. It is true in one direction only.

285 g against 275 g: ten grams for the dial

Two shoes on the same platform, two different weights.

Mizuno's Singapore product page for the dial model gives the full spec: style code 81GA2692, 285 g, made in Vietnam, upper 55% synthetic and 45% textile, outsole 90% rubber and 10% EVA, width listed as Men's-D. Mizuno Europe lists the lace model 81GA2582 at 275 g.

Ten grams sounds trivial until you place it on the line. The lightest shoe in this family, the Wave Drive EL2, is 225 g. The heaviest is this 285 g dial model. It is not "the same shoe with convenience added". It is the heaviest shoe in the range.

The weight has a physical source: the dial body, its cartridge mount, and the TX4 textile lace, which BOA notes uses Dyneema fibre. Convenience and mass are traded directly here.

Colourways move the number as well: the same retailer lists a limited colourway of 81GA2582 at 15,400 yen. Same style code, same shoe, different price. That is normal in Japanese shoe retail.

Three calls: when the dial is worth paying for

Compress the above into three usable rules.

First, if your reason is "I want to loosen a little when my foot swells mid-match", buy laces. L6 has no partial release, so the money does not buy the thing you want.

Second, if your reason is "fast on and off" and "the lace will not come undone during a lateral push", the dial works. Pull up, step in, press and wind. No bow tying, no re-lacing mid-game. Laces working loose during hard side-stepping is a real annoyance.

Third, if your foot is 3E, the question does not exist. The dial model offers no wide option, which I will show below.

Three checks before you order

First, the variant table is the size and width list. Titles carry the model name; tables carry centimetres and 2E or 3E. Of the five shoes here, three are 2E only, one is 3E only, and one carries a single size. None of that is visible from the title.

Second, "残りN点" is per variant, not per product. Each size has its own remaining count. Reading "残り1点" as "only one pair left in the whole listing" will mislead you in both directions.

Third, currencies get mixed. In one session, four links showed EUR and one showed JPY, depending on seller and delivery settings. That is not a broken page. Check the cart's actual currency before paying; every price in this article is a snapshot at the time of writing, for comparison only, not a quote.

On the tax line: Japan's personal import duty and consumption tax are waived below a taxable value of 10,000 yen, but shoes are computed separately, leather footwear is excluded from that waiver, and the Ministry of Finance amendment abolishes the small-parcel exemption for personal imports from 1 April 2028. Those are the customs and ministry positions; the actual determination is made at clearance.

FAQ

Q: Can the BOA dial be loosened quickly mid-match?

A: It can be released, but not loosened slightly. The L6 fitted here is a single-direction mechanism: the official actions are tighten and pull up for full release. Partial loosening means pulling the dial up and rebuilding tension, which is slower than slacking one lace run.

Q: Is 10 g between 285 g and 275 g noticeable on court?

A: Not on its own. But this family spans 225 g to 285 g, and the dial model sits at the top. If low weight is your priority, those 10 g move you the wrong way; if on-off speed and even clamping matter more, they are a fair trade.

Q: The BOA model is not on Amazon Japan. Can I buy it from Mizuno Japan directly?

A: Mizuno sells it, but I could not obtain an orderable Japanese product page from here: jpn.mizuno.com returns a 403 to overseas IPs, which is geo-blocking rather than a missing page. Whether the official store ships abroad, and whether you need a forwarder, has to be confirmed on Mizuno's own site. I am not going to guess on their behalf.

Q: 2E and 3E differ by 6 mm. Should I just go 3E?

A: No. 3E is wider, not better. If your foot is not pinched in 2E, there is no reason to widen, and extra width lets the foot slide laterally. The order is: measure foot length for centimetres, then check which width your current shoes do not rub in, then choose 2E or 3E.

Q: Why do these listings show euros and yen at the same time?

A: Amazon Japan displays currency based on delivery address and seller. EUR and JPY appeared side by side in my pull, which is normal. Prices are for comparison only; the cart is the authority.
