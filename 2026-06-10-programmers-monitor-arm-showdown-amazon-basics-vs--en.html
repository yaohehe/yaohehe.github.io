Before You Buy: Two Specs That Matter

1. Screen size + weight — Most arms claim 13-32 inch compatibility and 4.4-19.8 lb (2-9 kg) capacity. A 34-inch ultrawide with its stand typically weighs 6-8 kg, so go for a dual arm or a single arm rated for ≥ 20 lb.

2. VESA pattern — Your monitor must have 75x75mm or 100x100mm mounting holes on the back. iMacs and most ultra-thin laptops don't have VESA — you'll need a separate VESA adapter plate ($10-15), out of scope here.

FAQ — Pitfalls to Avoid

Q1: Is the Amazon Basics weight rating honest?

A: Based on community feedback, ratings are accurate, but a 32 inch 8kg screen sits right at the limit and needs extra tension screw turns. Leave 20% weight headroom (e.g., 8kg screen on a 10kg-rated arm).

Q2: My iMac has no VESA holes — what now?

A: iMacs (M1/M2/M3) don't have VESA on the back. Buy a separate VESA adapter plate ($10-15); all three arms in this guide support VESA 75/100.

Q3: Can a 34 inch ultrawide work on a single arm?

A: Not recommended. A 34 inch ultrawide with stand weighs 6-8 kg, hitting single-arm upper limits; the gas spring fatigues over time. Go dual arm (HUANUO FlowLift Dual) for proper per-arm weight distribution.

Q4: Gas spring vs mechanical spring — practical difference?

A: Big. Gas spring is one-hand (press a button, lift, release). Mechanical spring needs loosening a screw, lifting to position, re-tightening — 30 seconds vs 5 seconds per adjustment.

Q5: Can I mount on a glass desk?

A: Only if your glass desk has a non-glass edge frame. C-clamps need to grip 0.8-3.9 inches of non-glass material; glass edges are uneven and can crack under pressure. Glass desk users should default to wall mount or a grommet base (requires drilling).
