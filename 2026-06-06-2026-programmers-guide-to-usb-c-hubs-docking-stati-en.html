Why Programmers Need a USB-C Hub (Or Dock)

Thin-and-light laptops are here to stay. MacBook Pros ship with just two USB-C ports. Dell XPS has no ethernet. The moment you need a monitor, keyboard, mouse, ethernet, and an SD card reader—you need a hub.

The real difference between a $20 hub and a $100 dock comes down to three things: video output capability, power delivery, and number/type of ports.

FAQ

Q: Will a USB-C hub slow down my data transfers?

A: It depends on the hub's main chip and architecture. USB data ports typically operate independently from video output. However, if the hub uses USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode for video, video traffic shares the USB-C bandwidth and can affect USB speeds. Hubs labeled "USB 3.2 Gen 2" or "10Gbps" minimize this impact. UGREEN Revodok Pro's dual 10Gbps ports handle this well.

Q: Can I charge my laptop and use the hub at the same time?

A: Yes, via Power Delivery (PD). Most hubs advertise 100W or 85W PD—this is the power they can pass through to your laptop. Your laptop must support USB-C PD charging. MacBook Pro 16" needs 96W+, so a 100W hub is the safe choice for that machine.

Q: What do I need for dual monitors?

A: Two things: 1) The hub must have dual HDMI or HDMI+DP outputs; 2) Your laptop's USB-C port must support DisplayPort Alt Mode (almost all modern laptops do). Baseus 10-in-1 and Hiearcool 11-in-1 support dual monitors. UGREEN Revodok Pro and Anker 7-in-1 are single-HDMI only.
