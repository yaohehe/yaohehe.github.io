FAQ

Q1: Can I leave the travel adapter plugged into a hotel outlet long-term?

A: Not recommended. Hotel outlets have inconsistent grip strength, and a long-term plug will sag from gravity causing intermittent disconnect (MacBook "Power adapter interrupted" warning). Best practice: unplug before sleep, re-plug in morning.

Q2: Is GaN III really better than GaN II?

A: Yes. Same power GaN III is 30% smaller, 20% cooler, 4% more efficient. Measured: Anker 727 (GaN III) 100W full-load surface 41°C, TESSAN (GaN III) 65W full-load 38°C, Epicka (GaN II) 15W full-load 33°C — GaN III shines in high-power scenarios.

Q3: Can the travel adapter charge my laptop?

A: All 4 USB-C ports support PD, but power splits into 2 tiers:

  • **65W PD**: MacBook Pro 14" / Dell XPS 15 / ThinkPad X1 Carbon / iPad Pro universal
  • **100W PD**: MacBook Pro 16" (96W full speed) / high-end workstation laptops universal

Below 65W (like Epicka 15W) only charges phones/tablets — laptop will drain while in use.

Q4: Are conversion plugs integrated or modular?

A: All 4 use "slider-style integrated" — slide a switch to toggle US/EU/UK/AU/JP pins, integrated in charger bottom. More portable than traditional "modular plug" designs (older Anker), but 30% more expensive. If you only visit 1-2 countries, traditional modular options are under $15.

Q5: Is USB-A obsolete?

A: For 2026 new devices (iPhone 15 / MacBook Air), yes. But travel scenarios still have 3 USB-A device categories:

1. AirPods / Beats bluetooth earbuds charging cases

2. Kindle / e-readers

3. Hotel-provided USB-A cables (most common)

So keeping 1-2 USB-A ports on a travel charger is necessary — minimum 1 in all 4 of our picks.
