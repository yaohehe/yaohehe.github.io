Why Programmers Need a Footrest

I used a Herman Miller Aeron for 6 months and discovered an uncomfortable truth: even with the most expensive chair, if you set it to the correct ergonomic height (thighs parallel to floor), your feet dangle. Aeron's recommended seat height is 16-20.5 inches — but most desks aren't tall enough to match that. Result: feet dangle.

Dangling feet = continuous pressure on the back of the thigh = lower back compensates = chronic soreness. A footrest gives your dangling feet a support point, restoring the 90° thigh-to-floor angle.

Especially if you're under 175cm (5'9") tall with a standard 75cm fixed desk, you almost certainly need a footrest.

Q: Is a footrest a scam? Doesn't it conflict with a standing desk?

A: Standing desks don't need a footrest (standing = floor support). Sitting positions with a standing desk still need a footrest (you won't stand for 8 hours of coding). They don't conflict.

Q: I have chronic lower back pain. Will a footrest help?

A: A footrest mainly solves compensatory back pain from dangling feet. If your back pain is structural, the footrest is only a supplement — pair it with lumbar support and hourly standing breaks.
